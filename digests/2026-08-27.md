# PM + Fintech Daily Digest — 2026-08-27

## Fintech News

- **Embedded finance's regulators are catching up to the technology.** A widely-shared industry analysis published August 26 argues that embedded finance — lending widgets on e-commerce dashboards, insurance upsells inside rideshare apps — now rests on a three-layer accountability chain (platform, sponsor bank, program manager), and that most compliance disputes and enforcement actions originate from confusion over which layer owns which obligation. It's a sign the "invisible" embedded-finance boom is entering a more scrutinized, less permissive phase.
- **FDIC and OCC are both moving to speed up bank chartering.** The FDIC's revised two-phase deposit insurance review process (effective for applications received after August 15) now aims to give qualifying applicants contingent authorization within 120 days, while the OCC has publicly reaffirmed a priority to "reinvigorate" de novo chartering. Together they signal a deliberate regulatory push to make it easier for fintechs to become chartered banks rather than renting charters from partners.
- **SEC formally proposed new Regulation Crypto Assets on August 18.** The proposal reverses the agency's earlier shelving of a dedicated crypto-offering framework and would create clearer registration and disclosure rules for digital-asset issuers — a meaningful step toward the kind of regulatory clarity that's been a persistent blocker for institutional crypto products.
- **Dakota applied for a de novo national trust bank charter to support its stablecoin business.** The neobank, which launched an enterprise stablecoin offering earlier this year, filed with the OCC in early August — one more data point in the wave of fintechs seeking to own a bank charter directly rather than operate through a sponsor bank relationship.
- **Chime reported 27% year-over-year revenue growth in Q2.** The consumer neobank's continued double-digit growth, even amid a choppier fintech funding environment overall, underscores that scaled, profitable consumer fintech franchises are still finding room to expand.

## PM Concept of the Day: Change Control Board (CCB) / Formal Change Control

A Change Control Board is a formal governance body — typically product, engineering, compliance, and risk stakeholders — that reviews and approves or rejects proposed changes to a project's scope, schedule, or requirements once a baseline has been set, rather than letting changes get absorbed ad hoc. Each request is logged with its rationale, impact analysis (cost, schedule, risk), and a documented decision, which is exactly the kind of artifact PMP exam questions probe when they ask "what do you do when a stakeholder asks for an unplanned change mid-sprint." It matters more in regulated environments than almost anywhere else: today's news about the FDIC's new 120-day review process and the SEC's proposed Regulation Crypto Assets are exactly the kind of external regulatory shifts that should trigger a formal change request into a bank's or fintech's product roadmap — not a quiet Slack message to an engineer — because skipping that governance step is how compliance gaps and audit findings happen. A fintech PM shipping a stablecoin or crypto-custody feature would route any regulatory-driven requirement change through a CCB precisely so legal, compliance, and engineering all sign off on the same understanding of what changed and why.

## One Thing to Try Today

Pick one regulatory or policy change from today's news (e.g., the SEC's proposed Regulation Crypto Assets, or the FDIC's new 120-day deposit insurance timeline) and draft a one-page mock change request as if you were a PM at an affected fintech: state what's changing, which product/system it impacts, a rough cost/schedule impact estimate, and who would need to approve it. Spend 10-15 minutes — the goal is practicing how to translate an external regulatory event into the structured internal artifact a CCB would actually review.
