## PM Concept of the Day: Scope Baseline (Scope Statement + WBS + WBS Dictionary)

In PMBOK, the **scope baseline** is the approved version of a project's scope. It is one of three performance baselines, alongside the schedule and cost baselines, and it has three parts:

1. **Project scope statement:** what the project will deliver, the acceptance criteria, and, importantly, **what is out of scope**, plus assumptions and constraints.
2. **WBS:** the breakdown of the deliverables into work packages.
3. **WBS dictionary:** a detailed entry for each work package covering its description, owner, acceptance criteria and estimates.

The scope baseline can change only through formal change control. You check progress against it, and the PMP exam often asks what makes up the baseline, sometimes offering the requirements document or the charter as wrong answers.

Fintech example: imagine you're running a bank's pilot in an agentic-payments sandbox like the one MAS just announced. A tight scope statement might say: "Agents may start domestic payments up to S$1,000 using tokenised deposits. Out of scope: cross-border payments, stablecoin rails, and consumer onboarding." The WBS dictionary entry for "Agent identity verification" would then name its owner (the fraud and identity team), its acceptance criteria (every agent payment is checked against a KYA credential and the customer's authority limit), and the regulators' evidence requirements. If a sponsor asks mid-pilot to "just add stablecoins," you can point to the out-of-scope line and send the request through change control instead of absorbing it.

## One Thing to Try Today (10 minutes)

Write a one-paragraph **scope statement** for a small fintech feature, such as "let customers freeze their debit card in the app." Include exactly three in-scope items, three **out-of-scope** items, one assumption, and one acceptance criterion written so it can be tested. Then pick one work package and write its WBS dictionary entry: description, owner role, acceptance criteria and a rough estimate. The out-of-scope list is usually the hardest part to write and the most useful, so practise it until you can produce one quickly.
