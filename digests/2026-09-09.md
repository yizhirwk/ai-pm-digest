# Daily PM + Fintech Digest — 2026-09-09

## Fintech Industry News

1. **EverBank and WaFd Bank agree to a $3.9 billion reverse merger.** Florida-based EverBank will merge into Seattle-based WaFd, which will rename itself EverBank Financial Corp and trade as EVBK on Nasdaq, creating a combined ~$75 billion-asset regional bank expected to close in early 2027.

2. **FNZ raises $450 million in new equity.** The wealthtech infrastructure giant's round was the single largest fintech deal of the week (part of a ~$1.36 billion week across 12 deals), underscoring continued investor appetite for back-office wealth-management infrastructure even as overall deal counts stay modest.

3. **Federal Reserve staff examine where stablecoins fit in the money supply.** A Fed staff note published this week analyzes whether GENIUS Act-compliant payment stablecoins should be classified inside M1 or non-M1 M2, a technical-sounding question with real implications for how banks and regulators treat stablecoin float going forward.

4. **Ghana, Mauritius, and Uganda commit to coordinated stablecoin frameworks.** The three countries agreed to build common licensing regimes, reserve requirements, and cross-border payment standards for stablecoins designed to integrate with Africa's mobile-money ecosystem — an early sign of regional (rather than country-by-country) stablecoin regulation taking shape outside the US/EU/UK axis.

5. **G20 finance ministers back clearer digital-asset rules, but stablecoins are notably excluded.** The G20's September 1 statement endorsed stronger regulatory and supervisory frameworks for digital assets generally, while the Financial Stability Board continues a separate, still-unfinished review of global stablecoin arrangements — a reminder that "crypto regulation" and "stablecoin regulation" are moving on different international tracks.

## PM Concept of the Day: WSJF (Weighted Shortest Job First)

WSJF is a prioritization technique from the Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe) that ranks backlog items by dividing their **Cost of Delay** (the economic value lost by *not* shipping something sooner, combining user/business value, time criticality, and risk-reduction/opportunity-enablement) by the **job size** (a relative estimate of effort). The item with the highest Cost-of-Delay-to-size ratio goes first, because it captures the value the team is leaving on the table for every sprint it's deferred — not just how valuable or how big something is in isolation. It's a favorite SAFe/Agile exam topic because it forces an explicit accounting of time criticality, which a simple value-vs-effort matrix (like RICE, covered here in August) tends to understate. A fintech example: a regulatory-deadline item like GENIUS Act stablecoin compliance controls has extremely high time criticality (a hard external deadline with penalty risk), so even if it's not the "biggest value" feature on the roadmap, its WSJF score can rightly outrank a larger discretionary feature that has no deadline pressure — exactly the kind of judgment call PMP/SAFe scenario questions test.

## One Thing to Try Today

Take one item currently sitting near the top of your backlog and write down its Cost of Delay in three separate lines — user/business value, time criticality, and risk-reduction/opportunity-enablement — then divide by a rough T-shirt-size effort estimate. Compare that WSJF-style score against your gut-feel ranking of the same item; if they disagree, that gap is worth 10 minutes of thought about whether a hidden deadline or risk factor is being under-weighted.
