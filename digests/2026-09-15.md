## PM Concept of the Day: Servant Leadership

Servant leadership is the leadership model underlying Scrum and most Agile frameworks: instead of directing the team, the PM/Scrum Master's job is to remove impediments, shield the team from distraction, and create the conditions for the team to do its best work. It's a deliberate inversion of traditional command-and-control project management, where the PM's authority came from controlling scope, schedule, and budget. In a fintech delivery context, this looks like a PM negotiating with compliance and security teams to unblock a stuck PCI-DSS review, or clearing scheduling conflicts so engineers can focus on a payment-reconciliation bug instead of attending status meetings. PMP exam questions often test this by asking what a PM should do when a team is blocked — the servant-leadership answer is almost always "remove the obstacle," not "escalate for more control."

## One Thing to Try Today

Pick one fintech product you use (a banking app, a payments API, a BNPL checkout flow) and write down the single biggest "impediment" you imagine its delivery team faced before launch — a regulatory approval, a third-party integration, a security review. Then jot one sentence on what a servant-leader PM could have done to clear that obstacle faster. This builds the habit of thinking about delivery friction, not just features, when evaluating fintech products.
