## PM Concept of the Day: RACI Matrix

A RACI matrix maps every key decision or deliverable in a project against the people involved, assigning each one exactly one **Responsible** (does the work), one **Accountable** (owns the outcome and signs off — only one per item), and any number of **Consulted** (gives input beforehand) or **Informed** (told after the fact) parties. It's a core PMP communications-planning tool because ambiguity over "who actually owns this" is one of the most common causes of dropped deliverables and finger-pointing on cross-functional projects. The MVB Bank/Bretton AI deal above is a textbook case for why this matters in fintech: once an outside AI vendor is completing AML alerts and KYC cases, the bank's compliance officer must still be the sole **Accountable** party to the regulator — the vendor is **Responsible** for producing the analysis, but sign-off, escalation, and audit-trail ownership can't quietly shift to a third party just because the labor did. A PM standing up that vendor integration would build the RACI matrix before writing a single line of the workflow, precisely so "who's Accountable when FinCEN asks a question" is answered in writing, not discovered during an exam.

## One Thing to Try Today

Pick a process you'd be handing off to a vendor or another team (real or hypothetical — e.g., "an AI vendor now handles first-pass KYC alert review"). Spend 10 minutes listing the 4-5 concrete steps in that process and filling in a RACI matrix: one Responsible, one Accountable, and any Consulted/Informed parties per step. Notice any step where you instinctively want to write two names under Accountable — that's usually where a real project has a governance gap.
