# Daily PM + Fintech Digest — 2026-09-14

## Fintech News

- **TabaPay raises $155M and moves to acquire Transact Bank NA.** The US payments processor closed a strategic growth round led by FTV Capital and plans to use the proceeds to acquire the federally chartered, FDIC-insured Transact Bank, a move toward owning more of its banking-as-a-service stack rather than relying solely on partner banks. Closing is expected in Q4 2026, pending regulatory approval.
- **Félix lands $200M to expand cross-border payments into lending and savings.** The paytech's raise combines an $87M equity round led by Andreessen Horowitz (with QED Investors, Castle Island Ventures, and others) and a $113M credit facility from General Catalyst, signaling investor appetite for remittance-first platforms that broaden into adjacent financial products.
- **21 major banks — including Goldman Sachs, Citi, Bank of America, Deutsche Bank, and UBS — announced plans to jointly issue a USD-denominated stablecoin**, targeting a market launch in the first half of 2027. It's one of the clearest signs yet that incumbent banks intend to compete directly with Tether and Circle rather than cede stablecoin rails to crypto-native issuers.
- **TD Bank completed a live trial of tokenized asset settlement** under Project Agorá, routing real US dollar funds between TD Bank NA and its New York branch over a shared blockchain ledger with Bank of New York acting as clearing intermediary — a concrete step toward tokenized wholesale settlement rather than a lab pilot.
- **Stablecoin regulation is converging but still fragmented:** G20 finance ministers backed clearer digital-asset supervisory frameworks on September 1, yet stablecoins were explicitly excluded from that commitment days later while the Financial Stability Board continues its review. Singapore's central bank opened a public consultation on its own stablecoin licensing regime the same week, underscoring that global rules are still being written jurisdiction by jurisdiction.

## PM Concept of the Day: ADKAR Change Management Model

ADKAR (Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement) is a change-management framework that focuses on the individual, not the project plan — the idea being that organizational change only sticks when each affected person moves through all five stages. In fintech IT delivery, this matters most during system cutovers: a new KYC/AML screening tool or core banking migration can be technically flawless and still fail if frontline staff never move past "Awareness" into genuinely wanting ("Desire") and being able ("Ability") to use it. A PM applying ADKAR would pair the technical rollout plan with targeted communications, hands-on training, and post-launch reinforcement (metrics, coaching, quick wins) rather than assuming a single announcement email covers "change management." It's a useful complement to a RACI matrix or cutover plan, which cover *who does what* but not *how people adopt it*.

## One Thing to Try Today

Spend 10 minutes drafting a mini stakeholder list for a hypothetical project: "our company adds stablecoin settlement as a payment rail." List the 5-6 roles who'd need to sign off before go-live (e.g., compliance/AML, treasury, engineering, legal, customer support) and jot one sentence on what each would care about most. This builds the habit of thinking cross-functionally before scoping any fintech feature that touches money movement or regulation.
