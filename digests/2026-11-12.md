## PM Concept of the Day: Hypercare (Post-Go-Live Stabilization)

Hypercare is a planned, time-boxed period right after go-live. During it, the project team stays on with extra support: tighter monitoring, faster triage, named escalation contacts and daily defect reviews. Only after that does ownership formally move to business-as-usual operations. It belongs in the transition and closing work of a project. It's planned up front with exit criteria, such as "no Sev-1 incidents for 10 business days" or "failed-payment rate below 0.1%", so it doesn't end on a fixed date or whenever the team gets tired. Canada's Real-Time Rail is a good example. Launching with eight participants and a transaction cap, while the big banks join over the following months, gives each onboarding wave its own hypercare window. At each one, fraud-service alerts, settlement reconciliation and participant incidents get close attention before volume grows. For a fintech PM, leaving out hypercare is a classic mistake: the project is "done" on launch day, while a payments system's riskiest days are its first few weeks with real money moving.

## One Thing to Try Today (10 minutes)

Pick a real or hypothetical launch, such as a new payment method or a vendor API cutover, and draft a one-page hypercare plan with four parts:

1. Duration
2. Three measurable exit criteria
3. Who is on the escalation rota and how fast they must respond
4. The single metric you'd check every morning

Then write one sentence on who formally signs off the handover to operations. That sign-off is the step teams most often forget.
