## PM Concept of the Day: RAID Log (Risks, Assumptions, Issues, Dependencies)

A RAID log is a single tracking document that separates a project's uncertainties into four categories: **Risks** (things that might happen), **Assumptions** (things you're treating as true without proof), **Issues** (things that have already happened and need resolution), and **Dependencies** (things you're relying on from outside your control). It's a staple PMP and Agile artifact because it forces a team to stop lumping "things that could go wrong" into one vague bucket and instead assign each item an owner and a next action. In fintech delivery, this distinction matters a lot: the UBS case above started as a *risk* (AML monitoring gaps), became an *issue* the regulator escalated when it wasn't remediated, and a PM sitting on that program would have needed a *dependency* entry the moment a fix relied on a vendor's transaction-monitoring upgrade. Keeping RAID items separate — rather than one big risk register — makes it much easier to spot which unresolved risks are quietly turning into issues before they become headlines.

## One Thing to Try Today

Spend 10 minutes drafting a mini RAID log (4 rows, one per category) for a hypothetical fintech feature — e.g., "add stablecoin payout option to a payments app." For each row, write one concrete entry and name who would own it (compliance, engineering, vendor, etc.). This is the kind of artifact you'd be expected to produce or maintain in a real IT/fintech PM role.
