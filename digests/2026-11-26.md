- **The UK FCA published its approach to agentic AI in financial services (Nov 24).** It tells firms to prepare for customers who bring their *own* AI agents. That means checking that an agent is involved, confirming the customer's consent and what the agent is allowed to do, deciding whether to accept payments the agent initiates, and keeping a way for customers to get help when something goes wrong. The FCA says firms and senior managers stay accountable even when the agent comes from a third party. It also says agent providers may need authorisation if their agents carry out regulated activities such as payments or credit broking. It is applying existing rules rather than writing new AI-specific ones, and plans a follow-up "policy sprint." For payments product teams, this turns "agent-initiated payment" into a set of concrete requirements (consent, permissions, liability, redress) that will need designing in the UK. ## PM Concept of the Day: Conflict Resolution Modes (Thomas-Kilmann)

The PMI exam (and the **Thomas-Kilmann model**) groups the ways people handle conflict into five modes. **Collaborate/problem-solve** (work through the underlying interests to reach a win-win; usually the best answer when time and trust allow). **Compromise/reconcile** (each side gives something up; quick, but nobody fully gets what they need). **Smooth/accommodate** (play down the differences and give way to keep the relationship). **Force/direct** (one party imposes the decision; sometimes necessary in an emergency or on a non-negotiable regulatory point). **Withdraw/avoid** (step back or postpone; useful only when the issue is trivial or emotions need time to cool). No mode is always right. The PM's job is to pick deliberately, based on the stakes, the time available, and the relationship, and the exam's "best" answer is almost always collaborate, unless the question tells you time or safety rules it out. Fintech tie-in: picture the FCA's agent guidance arriving at a UK bank. Product wants to accept agent-initiated payments to win customers, while Fraud and Compliance want to block them, as several US banks do. Forcing either outcome leaves one side quietly working against you. Collaborating gets to the interests behind each position (growth versus liability and redress), and those can often be met together, for example with read-only access now plus payments with a capped limit for verified agents.

## One Thing to Try Today (10–15 minutes)

Write a **two-column "positions vs. interests" note** for the conflict above (Product vs. Fraud/Compliance on agent-initiated payments). In the left column, write what each side *says* it wants, which is its position. In the right column, write *why*, which is its underlying interest (e.g., "acquire digitally native customers," "avoid unrecoverable fraud losses," "be able to show the FCA how customers get redress"). Then draft **one proposal of no more than three sentences** that meets at least one interest from each side. Save it as a STAR-format story for interview questions like "Tell me about a time you resolved a conflict between stakeholders."
