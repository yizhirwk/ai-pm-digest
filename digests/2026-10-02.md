## PM Concept of the Day: Little's Law

Little's Law states that average cycle time = average WIP ÷ average throughput. It holds for any stable flow system, which makes it a cornerstone of Kanban and flow metrics (and a fair game for Agile exam questions). If a payments team averages 12 items in progress and finishes 4 per week, average cycle time is about 3 weeks; to cut it without working faster, reduce WIP. In fintech delivery, this explains why a team juggling KYC, ledger and reconciliation tickets at once ships each one slowly — and why limiting WIP is often the cheapest way to speed up compliance-driven releases.

## One Thing to Try Today (10 min)

Pull your team's (or a personal project's) last 10 completed tickets. Write down the start and finish dates, compute the average cycle time, then count the average items in progress. Check whether Little's Law roughly holds, and note one WIP limit you'd propose.
