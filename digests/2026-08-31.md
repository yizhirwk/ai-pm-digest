# PM + Fintech Daily Digest — 2026-08-31

## Fintech Industry News

1. **SEC proposes "Regulation Crypto Assets."** On August 18, the U.S. SEC put out its first bespoke rulemaking for crypto offerings, ending nearly a decade of enforcement-led oversight. It creates two new registration exemptions — a "startup exemption" (up to $5M over four years) and a "fundraising exemption" (up to $75M per year) — plus a safe harbor once an issuer's managerial promises are complete. Comments are open for 60 days after Federal Register publication.

2. **Revolut rolls out its EURR euro stablecoin, exits USDT the same week.** Revolut began a phased EURR rollout on August 26 (starting in Portugal, Poland, and Denmark), issued via Stripe-owned Bridge's Luxembourg entity, with the token designed to hold €1.00. The timing is notable: Revolut stops supporting USDT across the EEA and Switzerland today, August 31, positioning EURR as a MiCA-compliant replacement for its ~80M European customers.

3. **ESMA's "Report Once" plan targets up to €1B in annual EU compliance savings.** The European Securities and Markets Authority published its final report on simplifying transaction reporting, aiming to reduce duplicate submissions across regulatory regimes — a direct regtech opportunity for firms building compliance infrastructure serving multiple EU reporting obligations from a single data pipeline.

4. **U.S. regulators coordinate on digital assets across three agencies.** The SEC's crypto rulemaking lands alongside the OCC's explicit engagement with the GENIUS Act (the U.S. stablecoin framework) and the CFTC's first Innovation Advisory Committee meeting — signaling a more unified federal posture on digital-asset oversight after years of agency-by-agency divergence.

5. **Fintech funding stays concentrated in payments and infrastructure, not broad-based.** Weekly deal trackers for late August show funding clustering heavily in digital payments and B2B fintech infrastructure (over 60% of deal volume in recent weeks), with notable rounds including Fasset's $68M Series C (AI-powered stablecoin neobanking, $1B valuation) — reinforcing that investors are backing "rails" and compliance tooling over consumer-facing apps.

## PM Concept of the Day: Resource Leveling vs. Resource Smoothing

Both are schedule-adjustment techniques for resolving over-allocated resources, but they trade off differently. **Resource leveling** resolves a conflict (e.g., the same two engineers assigned to overlapping tasks) by delaying tasks until resources free up — this can push out the project end date and often means abandoning the original critical path. **Resource smoothing** only adjusts activities within their existing float, so the finish date stays fixed but some flexibility to shift work is sacrificed. In a fintech delivery context, imagine your KYC integration and your PCI-DSS remediation both need the same two backend engineers in the same sprint: leveling would sequence them one after another (slipping the ship date), while smoothing would shuffle non-critical tasks around them to protect the release date without adding headcount. PMP exams frequently test whether a scenario calls for a fixed deadline (smoothing) or an acceptable schedule slip in exchange for resource sanity (leveling).

## One Thing to Try Today

Spend 10 minutes building a rough resource histogram for a real or hypothetical two-week sprint: list your 3-4 team members down one side, days across the top, and shade in which days each person is double-booked across tickets. Seeing the over-allocation visually — even in a spreadsheet — is the fastest way to internalize why leveling and smoothing exist as distinct tools rather than interchangeable "just reprioritize" advice.
