# AI/PM + Fintech Digest — 2026-09-21

## Fintech Industry News

- **Chime agrees to buy its bank partner, Stride Bank, for $590M.** The neobank is acquiring the nationally chartered Oklahoma bank it has relied on for over seven years, moving from a sponsor-bank/BaaS model to direct ownership of its own regulated infrastructure. Chime says it will keep total assets under $10B for now and expects the deal (subject to OCC and Fed approval) to close in the first half of 2027.
- **Bastion wins conditional OCC approval for a national trust bank charter.** The stablecoin infrastructure firm, previously operating under a New York trust charter, will become Bastion Platforms National Trust Company, bringing its USDC custody, wallet, and white-label issuance business under direct federal oversight — though the charter still bars it from taking deposits or making loans.
- **ECB's Lagarde reportedly blocked Binance's near-complete MiCA license in Greece.** Per Wall Street Journal reporting, ECB President Christine Lagarde personally pressed Greek regulators to halt the approval, citing Binance's 2023 U.S. Bank Secrecy Act settlement and concern that dollar-backed stablecoins on Binance's platform could undercut the ECB's digital euro ahead of its planned 2027 pilot.
- **dtcpay extends its Series A to $25M.** The Singapore-based payments company, which bridges stablecoins and traditional finance rails, grew its round as part of a broader $858M week across 21 fintech funding deals globally — a reminder that stablecoin-to-fiat infrastructure remains one of the most heavily funded niches in payments right now.

## PM Concept of the Day: MoSCoW Prioritization

MoSCoW is a prioritization framework that sorts backlog items into four buckets: Must-have, Should-have, Could-have, and Won't-have (this time). Unlike a simple ranked list, it forces explicit tradeoff conversations with stakeholders about what's genuinely non-negotiable versus merely desirable, which is especially valuable when a release is anchored to a hard external deadline. A fintech PM building toward a regulatory cutover — say, the items needed for PSD3/PSR compliance or supporting a new bank-charter integration like Chime's — would tag every control or feature as a Must (blocks go-live), Should (important but has a workaround), Could (nice-to-have polish), or Won't (explicitly deferred), so leadership signs off on scope cuts before a deadline forces them. The framework's real power is the "Won't-have" bucket: naming what you're deliberately not doing prevents silent scope creep from re-litigating the same feature every sprint.

## One Thing to Try Today

Open your current sprint or release backlog and spend 10 minutes tagging each item with M, S, C, or W (MoSCoW). Don't aim for precision — aim for a first pass that surfaces disagreement: any item you're unsure whether to call "Must" or "Should" is worth a two-minute conversation with your tech lead or product owner today, before it becomes a last-minute scope fight near the deadline.
