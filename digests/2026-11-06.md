## PM Concept of the Day: Brooks's Law

Brooks's Law, from Fred Brooks's *The Mythical Man-Month*, says that adding people to a late software project makes it later. New members need onboarding time from existing team members, and communication paths grow as n(n-1)/2, so coordination cost rises faster than capacity. In fintech, imagine a payments-gateway migration that is behind schedule: adding five engineers two weeks before a regulatory deadline would likely slow the core team who must explain the ledger, card-network certification steps and compliance constraints. Better options are cutting scope (see MoSCoW), removing blockers, or fast-tracking independent work packages. For exam purposes, link it to crashing: crashing only helps when tasks are separable and the added resources can be productive quickly.

## One Thing to Try Today (10 minutes)

Pick a project you know, or an open-source one. List its current team size, calculate the communication channels with n(n-1)/2, and then note in two sentences what would happen to that number if three people were added. Decide which work package, if any, could realistically absorb the new people without slowing the core team.
