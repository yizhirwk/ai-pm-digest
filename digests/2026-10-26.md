## PM Concept of the Day: The Agile Manifesto (4 Values and 12 Principles)

The Agile Manifesto (2001) states four value pairs: **individuals and interactions** over processes and tools, **working software** over comprehensive documentation, **customer collaboration** over contract negotiation, and **responding to change** over following a plan. The key exam nuance is that the items on the right still have value; the manifesto says the left side is valued *more*. It is backed by 12 principles, including early and continuous delivery, welcoming changing requirements, a sustainable pace, and regular reflection on how to become more effective. In fintech delivery, consider post-acquisition integration like Tradeweb/Trumid: a team can still keep the documentation a regulator requires (audit trails, control mappings) while delivering the integration in small working increments and adapting as the combined platforms' differences surface. PMP and PMI-ACP questions often test whether you can tell when a scenario honors a value (e.g., demoing working software to stakeholders) versus violating it (e.g., locking scope in a contract and ignoring feedback).

## One Thing to Try Today

Pick one project you've worked on or read about (the Tradeweb/Trumid integration works) and, in about 10 minutes, write one sentence for each of the four Agile values describing how that project honored it or where it didn't.
