- **The OCC published a supervisory framework for how examiners assess banks' use of AI, including agentic AI (Nov 4, OCC Bulletin 2026-54).** It is a resource for examiners, not a new rule, and the OCC says it creates no new supervisory expectations. It groups 16 components into six pillars: purpose, risk, control, oversight, resilience and assurance. Its central principle is that controls should get stronger as the potential harm from an AI agent's failure or misuse grows. It names BSA/sanctions compliance, consumer compliance, and hallucinated fees, rates or account status as heightened-risk areas. For anyone shipping AI features into a bank or a BaaS partner, this is effectively the checklist examiners will bring. ## PM Concept of the Day: PDCA (Plan-Do-Check-Act / the Deming Cycle)

PDCA is the continuous-improvement loop behind PMBOK's quality management and Agile's inspect-and-adapt:

- **Plan:** state a hypothesis and how you'll measure it.
- **Do:** run a small-scale change.
- **Check:** compare the results to the plan.
- **Act:** standardize the change if it worked, or adjust it and repeat.

On the PMP exam, it's the expected answer when a question describes improving a *process*, rather than fixing a one-off defect. It's also the idea behind the "living document" the OCC describes in its AI framework.

Fintech example: say your team ships an AI support agent for a bank. **Plan:** "Grounding answers in the fee-schedule API will cut fee-related hallucinations below 0.5%." **Do:** enable it for 5% of chats. **Check:** sample and audit those transcripts against the baseline. **Act:** if the target is met, roll it out and add the check to the release checklist. If not, revise the hypothesis and run the cycle again.

## One Thing to Try Today (10 minutes)

Open the OCC's six pillars (purpose, risk, control, oversight, resilience, assurance). Use any of the links in the first news item. Choose one AI feature you know or can imagine in a fintech product, such as a chatbot answering balance questions, an agent that pays invoices, or AI-assisted KYC review.

Write **one sentence per pillar** answering "what would an examiner ask about this feature?" For example, under *oversight*: "Who can switch the agent off, and how fast?" Then circle the pillar where your answer is weakest. That's the first item on your launch-readiness checklist. Turning a regulator's framework into concrete delivery questions is a core skill for an IT/fintech PM.
