## PM Concept of the Day: Earned Value Management (EVM)

Earned Value Management is a quantitative technique for tracking whether a project is on budget and on schedule by comparing three numbers at any point in time: Planned Value (what work you scheduled to have done by now), Earned Value (what you've actually completed, valued at its planned cost), and Actual Cost (what you've really spent). From these, a PM derives the Cost Performance Index (EV/AC) and Schedule Performance Index (EV/PV) — ratios below 1.0 mean you're over budget or behind schedule, and it's a heavily tested PMP calculation. The FCA's transaction-reporting overhaul above is a good fintech example: a bank's compliance IT program now has an 18-month implementation window with a hard April 2028 go-live, and a PM running that program would use EVM monthly to catch early whether the schema-rebuild work is falling behind its planned burn rate, long before the regulatory deadline is at risk. EVM matters more in regulated fintech delivery than in typical software projects because the "deadline" is often not negotiable — it's set by a regulator, not a product roadmap.

## One Thing to Try Today

Pick a task you're currently partway through (work or personal) and estimate three numbers for it: the % complete you planned to be at by today, the % you're actually at, and the % of budgeted time/money you've spent so far. Compute Earned Value ÷ Planned Value and Earned Value ÷ Actual Cost as rough percentages — that's a hands-on feel for CPI/SPI, the two numbers PMP exam questions build entire scenarios around.
