## PM Concept of the Day: Critical Chain Project Management (CCPM)

CCPM, developed from the Theory of Constraints, argues that traditional schedules fail not because individual task estimates are wrong but because "safety time" gets wasted through multitasking, student syndrome (starting late), and Parkinson's Law (work expanding to fill time). Instead of padding every task, CCPM strips individual buffers out of task estimates and pools that slack into a single "project buffer" at the end of the critical chain (the longest sequence of dependent tasks accounting for both task and resource dependencies), plus smaller "feeding buffers" where non-critical paths merge into it. In fintech delivery, this maps well onto regulatory-dependent workstreams: rather than each team padding its own estimate for "in case compliance review takes longer," a project delivering a new payments feature can pool that uncertainty into one buffer that's actively monitored — burn-rate on the buffer becomes an early warning signal, not a series of hidden delays baked into individual tasks. PMP exam context typically frames CCPM as a resource-constrained alternative to CPM/PERT, testing whether you recognize buffer management as the mechanism, not just "critical path with less padding."
