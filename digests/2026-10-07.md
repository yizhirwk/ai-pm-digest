## PM Concept of the Day: Cost of Quality (COQ)

Cost of Quality is the total cost of achieving, or failing to achieve, quality, and it splits into two groups. Conformance costs are prevention (training, design reviews, standards) and appraisal (testing, audits, inspections); non-conformance costs are internal failures (rework found before release) and external failures (defects found by customers, fines, lost trust). The PMP principle is that spending more on prevention and appraisal usually cuts the far larger failure costs. In fintech this is vivid: automated reconciliation tests on a payments release cost little, while a production bug that double-charges customers brings refunds, support load and regulator attention. A PM uses COQ to justify test automation and compliance reviews in the budget.

## One Thing to Try Today (10 min)

Recall one defect or rework episode from a project you know. List what it cost (hours, money, trust), then write down one prevention or appraisal step that would have caught it earlier and roughly what that step would have cost.
