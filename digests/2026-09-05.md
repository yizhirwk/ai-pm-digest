# PM + Fintech Daily Digest — 2026-09-05

## Fintech Industry News

1. **Revolut wins conditional OCC approval for a US national bank charter.** The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency granted preliminary conditional approval on September 2 for "Revolut Bank US, National Association," a branchless digital bank to launch in the first half of 2027 with roughly $95M in initial capital. Notably, the OCC withheld sign-off on FX trading, merchant acquiring, and foreign correspondent banking until Revolut separately qualifies for each — an unusually staged approval — and full launch still needs FDIC and Federal Reserve approval too.

2. **X moves all US creator payouts off Stripe onto its own X Money rails.** Effective September 2, income from X's Original Content Rewards Program and creator subscriptions for US-based creators now flows through X Money instead of Stripe, removing the prior $30 minimum withdrawal threshold. It's a notable case of a large platform pulling payment processing in-house rather than relying on a third-party PSP — non-US creators still use Stripe for now.

3. **India's NPCI is preparing a "Unified Agent Protocol" to let AI agents transact over UPI.** The framework would let users delegate payment authority to AI agents within preset spending limits, targeting low-value/high-frequency use cases like grocery orders first, with identity verification and audit trails built in to address fraud risk. It's expected to be unveiled at the 2026 Global Fintech Fest in Mumbai and is one of the first concrete regulatory frameworks anywhere for "agentic commerce."

4. **Eftsure acquires Relish to build an end-to-end payment-assurance platform.** Announced September 3, the deal combines Eftsure's payment verification with Relish's AI-powered vendor data validation and invoice processing, creating a single platform covering onboarding through payment across 35+ finance systems (SAP, Coupa, Workday, NetSuite, etc.). Deal terms weren't disclosed, but the company says the combined platform now protects $288B in annual enterprise payments.

## PM Concept of the Day: Stakeholder Power/Interest Grid

A power/interest grid is a stakeholder-analysis tool that plots each stakeholder on two axes — how much power they have to affect the project, and how much interest they have in its outcome — to decide how much time and what kind of engagement each one needs (manage closely, keep satisfied, keep informed, or monitor). It's a staple PMP topic because "engage all stakeholders equally" is a recipe for wasted effort; the grid forces a PM to be deliberate about where attention actually pays off. The Revolut item above is a clean real-world example: OCC, FDIC, and the Federal Reserve are all high-power stakeholders in the same launch, but each cares about a different slice of the business (chartering, deposit insurance, holding-company supervision) and Revolut's rollout plan effectively manages each one on its own track rather than treating "the regulators" as one bucket. Any IT/fintech PM running a regulated launch faces the same problem — legal, compliance, security, and multiple regulators all need "managed closely," but not on the same cadence or with the same information.

## One Thing to Try Today

Take a project you're on (or the Revolut example above as a stand-in) and sketch a 2x2 power/interest grid with just 4-6 stakeholders on it. For each one, write one line on what "managed closely" or "kept informed" concretely means for them this week — a meeting, a status email, a sign-off request. If you can't name a concrete action for a "high power" stakeholder, that's a gap in your engagement plan worth fixing before it becomes a blocker.
