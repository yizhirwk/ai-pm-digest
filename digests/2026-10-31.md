## PM Concept of the Day: Sensitivity Analysis & Tornado Diagrams

Sensitivity analysis is a quantitative risk-analysis technique that asks which uncertain inputs have the biggest effect on a project outcome, by varying one input at a time while holding the others at baseline. The results are usually drawn as a tornado diagram: horizontal bars sorted from the widest swing at the top to the narrowest at the bottom, so the shape looks like a tornado and the top bars show where risk-response effort should go first. It differs from Monte Carlo simulation, which varies all inputs together to produce a probability distribution of outcomes. In fintech delivery, a PM planning a payments-platform migration might test how the go-live date shifts if sponsor-bank certification slips two weeks, vendor API delivery slips one week, or QA capacity drops 20%, and discover the certification delay dominates. For the PMP exam, remember it is a tool of the Perform Quantitative Risk Analysis process and that its output helps prioritize which risks deserve mitigation.

## One Thing to Try Today (10 minutes)

Pick a hypothetical project, such as launching a new card-dispute feature in 8 weeks. List five uncertain inputs (for example compliance review time, vendor delivery, test-environment availability, team capacity, and partner-bank sign-off) and give each a pessimistic and optimistic value. Estimate roughly how many weeks each swing moves the launch date, then sort them into a hand-drawn tornado diagram. Note which top bar you would tackle first and why.
