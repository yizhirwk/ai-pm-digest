## PM Concept of the Day: Project vs. Program vs. Portfolio Management

PMI separates three levels. A **project** is temporary work that produces a specific deliverable, and it is judged on scope, schedule, and cost. A **program** is a group of *related* projects managed together because coordinating them produces benefits that running them separately wouldn't, so a program manager focuses on dependencies between projects and on delivering those benefits. A **portfolio** is the full set of projects, programs, and operations an organization funds. Portfolio management chooses and ranks that work against strategy and capacity, which means it decides *which* projects should exist, not *how* to deliver them. A common exam trap: when a question is about choosing between initiatives or moving funding, the answer is portfolio-level; when it is about managing interdependencies to deliver a shared outcome, it is program-level. Fintech tie-in: Highnote's charter push is a program, not a project. Its projects include the OCC/FDIC application, a core-banking and ledger build, BSA/AML and compliance staffing, capital raising, and moving selected card programs off partner banks. Each has its own plan, but none produces value until all of them line up for opening day. Above that, the portfolio decision is how much engineering capacity goes into the bank build versus new issuing products for current customers, who are still served through partner banks.

## One Thing to Try Today (10–15 minutes)

Take one initiative you know well, at work or from the news (e.g., "launch a fintech bank charter" or "add agentic checkout"), and sketch a **three-level map** on a single page. At the top, write the *portfolio* question: what else competes for the same money or people? In the middle, write the *program*: the shared benefit and the 4–6 projects it needs. At the bottom, pick **one** project and write its deliverable, its main constraint, and the one dependency it has on another project. Then write one sentence on who would own each level. This gives you a ready answer to the interview question "What's the difference between a project manager and a program manager?" with a real example.
