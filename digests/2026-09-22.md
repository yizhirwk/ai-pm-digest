# AI/PM + Fintech Digest — 2026-09-22

## Fintech Industry News

- **Bank of England's systemic stablecoin consultation closes today.** The BoE's public consultation on its draft Code of Practice for sterling-denominated "systemic" stablecoin issuers ends September 22, with the Bank aiming to finalize the Code by year-end. Notably, the BoE softened its earlier proposal by replacing hard per-holder issuance limits with a temporary issuance guardrail (initially £40bn per stablecoin product) — a sign regulators are trying to balance financial-stability caution against not choking off a market UK banks want to compete in.
- **ISO 42001 is emerging as the go-to AI governance benchmark for payments and banking.** As firms embed AI deeper into underwriting, fraud detection, and compliance workflows, ISO/IEC 42001 — a management-system standard covering AI a company builds *or* buys — is becoming the reference framework for proving after-the-fact accountability for AI decisions, rather than just pre-deployment model testing.
- **RegTech firm CleverChain partners with Cedar Rose (a Coface company) to automate AML due diligence across MENA.** The deal pairs CleverChain's AI due-diligence agents with Cedar Rose's regional data and KYC infrastructure, aiming to compress manual anti-money-laundering screening in Middle East/North Africa markets — part of a broader wave of RegTech vendors racing to productize AI-driven compliance rather than just AI-driven customer features.
- **India's Moneyview trims its IPO but still targets a ~$624M valuation.** The digital lending platform cut its fresh-issue size (from ₹1,500cr to ₹750cr) ahead of a September 24 open, even as FY2026 profit jumped 65% and revenue rose 43% — a reminder that profitable fintech lenders are choosing more conservative primary raises in the current IPO market, leaning on secondary sell-down instead of diluting further.

## PM Concept of the Day: Burndown vs. Burnup Charts

A burndown chart tracks remaining work against time, sloping toward zero as a sprint or release progresses — it answers "are we going to finish on time?" A burnup chart instead tracks *completed* work against a separate, visible total-scope line, which rises whenever scope is added. The critical difference: a burndown chart hides scope creep (if 10 points get added mid-sprint, the "remaining work" line just doesn't drop as fast, with no visible cause), while a burnup chart makes it obvious immediately, because the total-scope line visibly steps up. This distinction is exam-relevant for PMP/Agile because scenario questions often test whether a candidate recognizes scope change versus a team execution problem. In fintech delivery, this matters most on regulation-driven backlogs — a BoE Code of Practice or an AML control set can gain new requirements mid-quarter as guidance is finalized, and a burnup chart lets a PM show a steering committee "we didn't slow down, the target moved" instead of absorbing the blame for a flat burndown line.

## One Thing to Try Today

Pull up your current sprint or release tracker and check whether it plots a burndown or a burnup. If it's a burndown, spend 10 minutes manually charting the last 2-3 sprints as a burnup instead (completed work vs. total scope over time) — you're checking whether "scope crept" or "the team fell behind" actually explains any recent schedule slip, which is a distinction worth having on hand before your next status conversation.
