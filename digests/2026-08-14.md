## PM Concept of the Day: Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)

A Work Breakdown Structure decomposes a project's total scope into progressively smaller, manageable pieces — from major deliverables down to individual work packages small enough to estimate, assign, and track — following the "100% rule" that every level must fully capture the scope of the level above it with no gaps or overlap. It's one of the most heavily tested PMP planning artifacts because a WBS built well up front is what makes later estimating, scheduling, and cost-tracking (including EVM, covered a few days ago in this digest) actually reliable, rather than guesswork. Mastercard's BVNK integration above is a good illustration of why this matters at scale: "integrate BVNK's stablecoin rails into Mastercard's network" isn't a single task — a PM would decompose it into work packages like regulatory licensing transfer, API/settlement integration, fraud-and-compliance control mapping, and customer migration, each owned by a different team with its own schedule. Skipping that decomposition is exactly how integration programs quietly balloon — a work package nobody named (like reconciling two companies' AML monitoring systems) is scope that falls through the cracks until it becomes a fire drill.

## One Thing to Try Today

Pick one of today's news items — e.g., "migrate a bank's stablecoin settlement rails onto an acquirer's network" — and spend 10-15 minutes sketching a 2-level WBS: 4-6 major deliverables, then 2-3 work packages under each. Check your own work against the 100% rule: does the sum of your work packages actually cover the whole deliverable, with nothing missing and nothing double-counted? This decomposition habit is the single most useful planning skill for scoping any IT/fintech project before you touch a schedule or budget.
