## PM Concept of the Day: Spikes (Time-Boxed Research Stories)

A spike is a time-boxed story in Agile/XP used to reduce uncertainty, through research or a throwaway prototype, before committing to estimating or building a feature. Its output is knowledge (a decision, a recommendation, a proof of concept), not shippable code, and it has a fixed limit such as one or two days. For example, before promising a client that your platform can plug into a new instant-payments rail, a team might spike for two days to test the sandbox API and learn the message formats, settlement timing and error handling. This turns a vague, risky estimate into a grounded one, and on the exam it is a classic technique for handling technical risk and unknowns.

## One Thing to Try Today (10 min)

Pick one upcoming feature or integration you're unsure about, whether at work or in a side project. Write a one-paragraph spike: the question to answer, a time box (e.g., 1 day), and what "done" looks like (a decision, not code). Note which risk it retires.
