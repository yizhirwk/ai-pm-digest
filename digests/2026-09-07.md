# PM + Fintech Daily Digest — 2026-09-07

## Fintech Industry News

1. **Revolut wins conditional OCC approval for a US national bank charter.** On September 3, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency gave Revolut preliminary approval to form "Revolut Bank US, National Association," though it still needs FDIC deposit insurance and Federal Reserve sign-off before opening; retail foreign-exchange activity is explicitly excluded from this approval and needs separate clearance. Executives say they're still targeting a first-half-2027 launch, ending Revolut's earlier plan to acquire an existing US lender instead.

2. **TabaPay raises $155M to buy its own bank charter.** The payments processor closed a growth round led by FTV Capital on September 2 and simultaneously announced plans to acquire Denver-based Transact Bank, N.A., renaming it "TabaBank, N.A." once the Q4 2026 deal closes. It's a notable shift for a payments company to own bank infrastructure directly rather than lean entirely on partner-bank relationships for money-movement rails like RTP, FedNow, ACH, and card sponsorship.

3. **OpenPayd lines up a $1.145B Nasdaq listing via SPAC merger with Titan Acquisition Corp.** The UK payments-infrastructure provider has been integrating MSB USA's 43 state money-transmitter licenses ahead of the deal's expected Q4 2026 close, giving it a regulated US payments footprint before it trades publicly under ticker "OP" — one of several fintech infrastructure players using the current SPAC window to go public.

4. **Wultra raises €6.8M Series A for post-quantum bank authentication.** The Series A, led by Seventure Partners, funds Wultra's push into passwordless, phishing-resistant digital identity for banks and fintechs preparing for both AI-driven fraud and the eventual post-quantum cryptography transition, alongside compliance with upcoming PSD3/PSR and eIDAS 2.0 requirements in Europe.

5. **UK opens its formal cryptoasset authorisation window on September 30.** Under the new FSMA-based crypto regime, firms currently operating under transitional Money Laundering Regulations registration have a five-month window (through February 28, 2027) to file for full FCA authorisation; applying inside the window lets a firm keep operating under savings provisions while its application is assessed, versus facing restricted promotions if it misses the deadline.

## PM Concept of the Day: SLAs, SLOs, and SLIs

These three terms get used interchangeably but describe different layers of a reliability commitment. An **SLI** (Service Level Indicator) is the actual measurement — say, the percentage of payment API calls that complete in under 300ms. An **SLO** (Service Level Objective) is the internal target for that indicator (e.g., 99.9% of calls under 300ms) that engineering and product use to make trade-off decisions. An **SLA** (Service Level Agreement) is the external, often contractual, promise to a customer or partner — usually looser than the internal SLO, with defined penalties (credits, termination rights) if breached. TabaPay's move to acquire its own bank charter above is partly an SLA story: owning the rail gives it more control over the SLIs that feed its uptime and settlement-time SLOs, rather than inheriting whatever a partner bank can guarantee.

For an IT/fintech PM, the practical discipline is keeping SLO targets stricter than the SLA you've promised externally — that gap is your error budget, the buffer that lets you ship changes and absorb incidents without immediately breaching a customer contract.

## One Thing to Try Today

Pick one API or service your team owns (or a payment/banking API you rely on, like a card-processing or KYC vendor). In 10 minutes, write down: the SLI you'd actually measure, the internal SLO target, and the external SLA if one exists publicly. If you can't find a documented SLA, that's itself a gap worth raising with your team.
