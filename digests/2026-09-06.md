# PM + Fintech Daily Digest — 2026-09-06

## Fintech Industry News

1. **FIS launches an Embedded Banking Platform for US banks.** Announced September 3, the platform lets banks deliver accounts and payments directly inside the business software their corporate customers already use — via API, SDK, widget, or white-label app — while keeping customer funds on the bank's own balance sheet rather than a third-party ledger. Cogent Bank, Commercial Bank of California, and M&T Bank are piloting it, with account-opening and payments capability targeted for Q4 2026; it's a notable bid to keep banks (not just sponsor-bank fintech middlemen) at the center of embedded finance.

2. **Singapore's MAS proposes a formal stablecoin licensing framework.** The Monetary Authority of Singapore published draft amendments to the Payment Services Act on September 1 to create a legal basis for regulating stablecoins issued in Singapore, joining a widening list of jurisdictions (Thailand, the EU, UK, Hong Kong) writing bespoke stablecoin rules in 2026 rather than fitting them into existing payment-services law.

3. **Apate.AI raises $8.15M seed to fight scams with adversarial AI agents.** The Australian startup, which deploys AI personas that autonomously waste scammers' time and harvest threat intelligence, closed an oversubscribed round led by Lobby Capital and has reincorporated in the US as it expands beyond existing clients like Commonwealth Bank of Australia. It's an example of fraud prevention shifting from purely defensive detection to actively engaging attackers with AI at scale.

4. **Nasdaq completes its acquisition of AI due-diligence platform Dasseti.** First announced in July and closed this week, the deal folds Dasseti's AI-driven due-diligence-questionnaire and monitoring tools (covering 17,000 asset managers and GPs representing $34T in AUM) into Nasdaq eVestment, extending Nasdaq's regtech/compliance-tooling footprint into ongoing manager monitoring, not just onboarding checks.

## PM Concept of the Day: The Kano Model

The Kano Model sorts product features into categories based on how they affect customer satisfaction: "must-be" features whose absence causes dissatisfaction but whose presence isn't noticed (e.g., basic security), "performance" features where more is linearly better (e.g., faster payment settlement), and "delighters" that create disproportionate satisfaction when present but aren't expected (e.g., an unexpectedly slick UX). It's a favorite PM interview and exam topic because it corrects the instinct to treat all backlog items as equally prioritizable — a must-be feature isn't optional just because it doesn't show up as a "delight" score. FIS's embedded banking platform above illustrates this well: keeping funds on the bank's own balance sheet is a must-be for regulated banks (its absence would be disqualifying), while the API/SDK/widget flexibility is more of a performance feature that differentiates FIS from other embedded-finance vendors.

A fintech PM building a compliance-heavy product should map their backlog against Kano before arguing about priority: confirm every must-be is already committed (they're non-negotiable, not up for a stack-rank debate), then spend the actual prioritization energy on performance and delighter trade-offs.

## One Thing to Try Today

Take your current backlog (or the FIS example above) and sort 5-8 items into must-be, performance, or delighter. For each must-be item, check whether it's actually tracked as "already required" rather than sitting in the same prioritization queue as the delighters — if it isn't, that's a process gap worth flagging to your team.
