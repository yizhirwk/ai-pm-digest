## PM Concept of the Day: Kanban WIP Limits (Work-in-Progress Limits)

Work-in-Progress (WIP) limits are a core Kanban practice that caps how many items a team can have active in any given workflow stage (e.g., "In Review" can hold at most 3 tickets) at one time. The goal is to force finishing over starting: when a column is full, the team must help clear it before pulling new work, which exposes bottlenecks immediately instead of letting half-done work quietly pile up. This maps directly onto fintech delivery, where a compliance-review or security-sign-off stage is often the true bottleneck — as N26's stalled buyout round and repeated BaFin interventions illustrate, unlimited work queued in front of a slow gate (regulatory sign-off, AML remediation) just accumulates risk rather than getting resolved. A PM enforcing WIP limits on a "Compliance Review" column would rather block new feature starts than let five unreviewed items sit indefinitely.

## One Thing to Try Today

Look at your team's current board (Kanban, Jira, Trello, whatever you use) and count how many items are sitting in your slowest or most bottlenecked column right now. Set an explicit WIP limit for that column today and note what happens to the flow over the next day or two — even a rough limit reveals whether "waiting on review/compliance/approval" is your real constraint.
