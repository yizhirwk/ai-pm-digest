## PM Concept of the Day: Analogous vs. Parametric vs. Bottom-Up Estimating

PMBOK names several ways to estimate cost and duration. Each trades accuracy against effort:

| Technique | How it works | Accuracy / effort | Best used when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Analogous** (top-down) | Scale from a similar past project using expert judgment | Low accuracy, very fast | Early on, little detail is known (e.g., charter, ROM estimate of −25% to +75%) |
| **Parametric** | Multiply a statistical unit rate by a quantity (e.g., hours per API endpoint × number of endpoints) | Medium–high accuracy, if the historical data is solid and the work scales linearly | Repeatable, measurable work |
| **Bottom-up** | Estimate each work package in the WBS and roll the totals up | Highest accuracy, slowest | Scope is broken down well enough to plan in detail (definitive estimate, roughly −5% to +10%) |

A typical PMP exam question gives a scenario such as "early phase, limited information, sponsor wants a quick number" and expects **analogous**. If the scenario says "a reliable historical cost per unit is available," the answer is **parametric**.

Fintech example: at kickoff for a new card-issuing integration, you might give the steering committee an analogous estimate: "our last processor migration took 9 months." Once you know there are 40 ISO 8583 message types to map, and your team's history is about 3 days per message type, you can switch to a parametric estimate. When the WBS is complete, roll up bottom-up estimates per work package, including certification testing with the card scheme, before you commit to a launch date.

## One Thing to Try Today (10 minutes)

Pick a piece of work you know, or invent one, such as "add Apple Pay to a banking app." Estimate it three ways. (1) **Analogous:** one number based on a similar project you've seen. (2) **Parametric:** find a countable unit (screens, endpoints, test cases) and a per-unit rate. (3) **Bottom-up:** list 5–8 tasks and estimate each one. Write the three totals side by side, then note in one line why they differ and which one you'd give a sponsor *today*. Explaining which estimate you'd use at each project stage is a common interview and PMP exam question.
