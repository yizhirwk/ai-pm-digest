# Daily PM + Fintech Digest — 2026-09-23

## Fintech News Roundup

- **CFTC signals a push toward "mass tokenization" and 24/7 markets.** CFTC Chair Michael Selig said on September 22 that U.S. regulators need to prepare markets for round-the-clock trading and much broader use of tokenized assets and stablecoins, a signal that market-structure rules may shift faster than firms expect.
- **Crypto market-structure bill stalls in the Senate.** The CLARITY Act, meant to set clear rules for digital-asset market structure, failed a Senate cloture vote 49-50 on September 15, with stablecoin-rewards provisions and conflict-of-interest concerns cited as sticking points — leaving regtech teams without the regulatory clarity they'd been building toward.
- **Twenty-one major banks move to launch a joint dollar stablecoin.** Goldman Sachs, Citi, Bank of America, Deutsche Bank, UBS, and other large banks and asset managers announced on September 1 that they're forming a consortium to issue a USD-pegged stablecoin, targeting a market launch in the first half of 2027 — a direct challenge to Tether and Circle's roughly 85% share of the ~$300B stablecoin market.
- **Félix raises $200M to scale AI-powered remittances.** The Miami-based WhatsApp remittance platform for Latino immigrants closed a $200 million Series C on September 1, underscoring continued investor appetite for AI-driven consumer payments infrastructure in underserved corridors.
- **Abwab.ai raises a seed round to become an "intelligence layer" for SME lending.** The startup raised $4 million (reported September 22) to apply AI underwriting and data tooling to small-business lending decisions, part of a broader wave of embedded-finance and lending-infrastructure startups targeting the SME gap.

## PM Concept of the Day: Cumulative Flow Diagram (CFD)

A Cumulative Flow Diagram is a stacked area chart that tracks how many work items sit in each stage of your workflow (e.g., To Do, In Progress, Review, Done) over time, making bottlenecks visible as a widening band in the chart rather than a gut feeling. In a fintech delivery context, a CFD is especially useful for spotting when items are piling up in a "Compliance Review" or "Security Sign-off" column before a regulatory deadline — the kind of stall that's easy to miss on a standup but obvious once plotted. Unlike a burndown chart, which only shows total remaining work, a CFD reveals *where* in the pipeline work is getting stuck, so a PM can act on the actual constraint (adding reviewer capacity, splitting tickets) instead of just pushing the team to "go faster." It's a core tool for Kanban-style flow metrics and shows up on Agile/PMI exams under flow-based and lean delivery topics.

## One Thing to Try Today

Open your team's Kanban or sprint board right now and count how many items are sitting in whatever column represents review, QA, or compliance sign-off. If it's more than 3-4 items, that's an early bottleneck signal — jot a one-line note (which column, how many items, since when) so you have a data point ready before it becomes a missed deadline conversation.
