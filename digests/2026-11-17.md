## PM Concept of the Day: The Daily Scrum (and why it isn't a status meeting)

The Daily Scrum is a 15-minute event, held every working day of the sprint, for the Developers. Its purpose is to inspect progress toward the **Sprint Goal** and adapt the plan for the next day. The 2020 Scrum Guide dropped the "three questions" (yesterday/today/blockers) as a required format. Developers may use any structure that focuses the conversation on the goal, and detailed problem-solving moves to a follow-up right after.

On the PMP and PSM exams, the usual trap is a manager or Scrum Master running it as a status report. The correct answer is usually: the Developers own it, the Scrum Master makes sure it happens and stays within the timebox, and impediments get removed *after* the meeting, not during it.

Fintech example: a team is two days from a card-issuing release, and the sanctions-screening integration is stuck waiting on a vendor's sandbox. A status-style standup produces ten updates and no decision. A goal-focused one asks, "Is the Sprint Goal still reachable?" Then two developers swarm on a mock screening service, and the PM escalates to the vendor at 9:16, a minute after the timebox ends.

## One Thing to Try Today (10 minutes)

Pick the next standup or recurring check-in you attend, or think back to the last one. Write down every update that was said, then mark each one as either **(G)** it changed what someone will do toward the goal, or **(S)** status only.

Count the S's. Then draft a one-line opening question you could propose to replace the round-robin. For example: "What's the biggest risk to [this sprint's goal] today, and who's on it?" Being able to turn a meeting from reporting into decisions is one of the most visible skills a new IT/fintech PM can show.
