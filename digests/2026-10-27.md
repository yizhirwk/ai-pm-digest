## PM Concept of the Day: Sprint Planning and the Sprint Goal

Sprint Planning is the Scrum event that opens each sprint. The team answers three questions: why is this sprint valuable (the **Sprint Goal**), what can be done (the items pulled from the Product Backlog), and how will the work get done (a plan for turning those items into a Done increment). The Sprint Goal is a single coherent objective that gives the team flexibility. If scope has to change mid-sprint, the team renegotiates the backlog items with the Product Owner but keeps the goal. For the exam, note that the Developers select how much work to take on, and the Product Owner doesn't assign it. In fintech delivery, take the Finastra cash management carve-out above. A sensible goal would be "a pilot customer can run cash positioning end to end on the separated platform". That goal ties the sprint to an outcome and lets the team drop a nice-to-have report if a data-separation problem eats capacity. A goal like "complete 14 tickets" doesn't give the team that guidance.

## One Thing to Try Today (10 minutes)

Pick one project you're working on or following, such as an open-source repo or a work initiative. Write one sentence stating what outcome the next two weeks of work should achieve, as a Sprint Goal. Then list three backlog items and mark which one you'd drop first if time ran short. If the sentence reads like a task list, rewrite it as an outcome.
