## PM Concept of the Day: Task Dependencies, Leads, and Lags

Schedule logic uses four dependency types. **Finish-to-Start** (FS) is the default: B can't start until A finishes. **Start-to-Start** (SS) means B can start once A has started. **Finish-to-Finish** (FF) means B can't finish until A finishes. **Start-to-Finish** (SF) is rare. A **lead** overlaps tasks by pulling the successor earlier (FS-2 days), and a **lag** forces a delay between them (FS+3 days). Dependencies are also classified as mandatory (hard logic, such as legal or physical constraints), discretionary (team preference or best practice) and external (outside the project). On the PMP exam, discretionary dependencies are the ones to look at first when fast-tracking. In fintech delivery, a sandbox-to-production payments integration might use FS with a lag, because a card network's certification takes a fixed number of days after the test submission. Load testing might use SS with the end of build, because it can start while build work finishes. A PM who labels each dependency by type can see which links can be overlapped and which are fixed by a regulator or vendor.

## One Thing to Try Today (10 minutes)

Pick a launch you know, or a hypothetical one such as a new payment method going live. List eight tasks and draw an arrow between each pair that depends on another. Label each arrow FS, SS or FF, and mark it mandatory, discretionary or external. Then circle one discretionary link you could overlap with a lead, and note the rework risk that would add.
