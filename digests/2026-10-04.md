## PM Concept of the Day: Scope Creep & Gold Plating

Scope creep is the uncontrolled growth of a project's scope without matching adjustments to time, cost or resources. It usually arrives through many small "can we just add..." requests. Gold plating is its quieter cousin: the team adds extras nobody asked for, and PMI treats it as poor practice because every extra feature adds risk and testing effort. Fintech makes both easy to fall into. Take the UK stablecoin regime above. A team building an issuer platform might add a multi-currency wallet or a loyalty feature because it seems cheap, while the regulated core, such as reserve reconciliation and AML controls, still has no slack in its schedule. The PMP-standard defence is a documented baseline scope, a formal change-control process that assesses every request for impact, and a habit of asking "which requirement does this serve?" before building anything.

## One Thing to Try Today (10 min)

Take a project you know, or a side project, and write down its scope in three lines: what is in, what is out, and what would count as a change request. Then list the last three "small additions" anyone asked for. Mark each one as either a justified change or creep, and note who should have approved it.
