# PM + Fintech Daily Digest — 2026-09-08

## Fintech Industry News

1. **21 of the world's largest banks form a joint venture to issue their own stablecoins.** Reported in early September, the consortium (built around a company sometimes referenced as "Currn") plans to pair tokenized deposits — which keep liquidity and customer relationships on bank balance sheets — with public-blockchain-facing stablecoins for cross-border payments and digital-asset settlement, a direct response to non-bank stablecoin issuers capturing payments volume.

2. **Bank of England and FCA consultation on systemic stablecoin oversight nears its close.** The joint proposal, open for comment through September 30, would have the FCA regulate all UK-issued qualifying stablecoins and retail payment use, while HM Treasury-designated "systemic" stablecoins get joint BoE/FCA supervision — a two-tier model UK fintechs will need to plan product roadmaps around.

3. **G20 finance ministers back clearer digital-asset rules but leave stablecoins out for now.** At their September 1 meeting, G20 finance ministers and central bank governors endorsed clearer regulatory and supervisory frameworks for digital assets generally, but stablecoins were explicitly excluded pending the Financial Stability Board's ongoing review of global stablecoin arrangements.

4. **Fintech funding stayed hot into September: $1.36B across 12 deals in one week.** WealthTech platform FNZ took the largest slice with a $450M equity raise; the rest of the week's deals skewed toward wealthtech, financial infrastructure, paytech and cybertech, including nine-figure international raises out of Indonesia and Mexico — a sign investor appetite hasn't cooled from H1's 23% funding surge.

5. **India's Global Fintech Fest 2026 opened in Mumbai.** The four-day event, running September 8–11, centers on quantum-readiness, agentic AI and asset tokenization, with Maharashtra's state government pushing initiatives (AI innovation parks, cybersecurity centers, digital public infrastructure) aimed at positioning Mumbai as a global fintech hub alongside London, Singapore and New York.

## PM Concept of the Day: Build vs. Buy Decision Analysis

Build vs. buy is the structured process of deciding whether to develop a capability in-house or acquire it externally (via vendor, partner, or M&A), weighing total cost of ownership, time-to-market, control, and strategic differentiation against each option. A PM typically scores both paths on cost, speed, maintenance burden, and whether the capability is a genuine competitive differentiator or plumbing that's better outsourced. Today's news is a live example: the 21-bank consortium chose to *build* shared stablecoin infrastructure rather than *buy* into an existing issuer's rails — betting that owning the tokenized-deposit layer is strategic enough to justify the build cost, much like TabaPay's decision earlier this month to buy a bank charter outright rather than keep renting one through a partner bank.

For an IT/fintech PM, the discipline is resisting the default answer (build, because engineers want to; or buy, because it's faster) and instead running the comparison explicitly — total cost over 3+ years, vendor lock-in risk, regulatory exposure, and whether the capability touches your core value proposition or is undifferentiated infrastructure.

## One Thing to Try Today

Pick one tool or service your team currently buys (a vendor API, a SaaS platform, a KYC/fraud provider) or one you built in-house. Spend 10 minutes listing three reasons the opposite choice (buy if you built, build if you bought) might have made sense at the time — this sharpens the habit of evaluating build-vs-buy as an ongoing decision, not a one-time commitment.
