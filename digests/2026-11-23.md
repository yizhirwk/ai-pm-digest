- **JPMorgan, Visa and Mastercard said attackers breached outside marketing vendors and leaked some customer contact data (reported Nov 19).** All three said their own networks and payment systems were not breached, and Visa said no card numbers were affected. The ransomware group n0n claimed responsibility. Researchers quoted by Reuters found about 16,000 customer email addresses, some JPMorgan records with phone numbers and account IDs (apparently Chase Reserve cardholders), and a jumbled set of Mastercard entries that may be test data. Reuters has not verified the data, and none of the firms named the vendor. The lesson for fintech teams: marketing and CRM vendors hold customer data too, and leaked contact data tends to come back as phishing and payment-redirection scams. ## PM Concept of the Day: Blameless Postmortem (Post-Incident Review)

A **blameless postmortem** is a structured review held after an incident. It asks *what in the system let this happen* and not *who made the mistake*. It assumes people acted reasonably given what they knew at the time. A typical write-up has a timeline, impact (customers, money, SLAs), contributing factors (often found with "5 Whys" or a fishbone diagram), what went well, and action items that each have an owner and a due date. The point is that people only report the full facts when they aren't afraid of punishment, and you can't fix what nobody reports. Fintech tie-in: after a vendor data leak like this week's, a blaming review stops at "marketing picked a bad vendor." A blameless one asks why customer account IDs were in a marketing dataset at all and why no data-minimization check caught it. That leads to fixes such as a data-classification step in vendor onboarding and tighter field-level sharing, which help every team, not just the one that got hit.

## One Thing to Try Today (10–15 minutes)

Write a one-page **mock postmortem** for this week's vendor breach, using only public facts. Use five headings: *Summary, Timeline, Impact, Contributing Factors, Action Items.* Under Contributing Factors, write at least two that are about systems or processes (for example, "no rule limiting which customer fields can go to marketing vendors"), and none that blame a person. Under Action Items, give each item an owner role (e.g., "Vendor Risk Lead") and a target date. Keep it as a portfolio sample. In interviews you can then show you know how a PM runs incident follow-up, not just a sprint.
