## PM Concept of the Day: Expected Monetary Value (EMV) & Decision Tree Analysis

Expected Monetary Value is a quantitative risk-analysis technique that converts uncertain outcomes into a single comparable number by multiplying each possible outcome's probability by its monetary impact and summing the results — a heavily tested PMP calculation for choosing between competing options under uncertainty. Decision trees extend this by mapping out sequential decision points and their branching outcomes, letting a PM compare the EMV of different paths (e.g., "build" vs. "buy") rather than relying on gut feel. This is exactly the calculus behind today's Fasset story: SBI Group's decision to lead a $68M round and later exercise warrants to become an equity-method affiliate is effectively a decision tree — the EMV of deeper ownership (higher stake in a $1B stablecoin platform, weighted by the probability that Fasset's cross-border rails keep scaling) has to be compared against alternative uses of that capital. A fintech PM sizing a build-vs-partner decision for a new payment rail or compliance system would use the same technique: assign probabilities to a vendor integration succeeding on time vs. slipping, multiply by the cost or revenue impact of each scenario, and let the EMV — not the loudest voice in the room — decide the recommendation.

## One Thing to Try Today

Pick one open decision on your plate right now (even a small one — which vendor to pilot, whether to build a feature in-house) and sketch a two-branch decision tree on paper: list the realistic outcomes for each option, assign a rough probability and dollar/time impact to each, and calculate the EMV for both branches. This takes 10-15 minutes and builds the habit of quantifying trade-offs instead of defaulting to intuition — a skill directly tested on the PMP exam and directly useful the next time you're arguing for budget in a fintech IT program.
