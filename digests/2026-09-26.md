## PM Concept of the Day: Cost of Delay (CoD)

Cost of Delay is a way of quantifying, in dollar terms, what it costs the business for every week or sprint a piece of work sits unshipped — combining the value the feature would generate, any urgency/time-decay in that value, and the risk being carried by not shipping it. It's central to SAFe's WSJF prioritization but is useful on its own: a $50k/week Cost of Delay makes the tradeoff of pulling in one more sprint of polish immediately concrete, versus a vague "priority: high" label. In fintech delivery, CoD is a natural fit for compliance-driven or regulatory-deadline work — for example, a KYC control that closes a fraud-detection gap has a Cost of Delay that spikes sharply as a known regulatory or audit deadline approaches, which should push it ahead of a backlog item with steady but low ongoing value. Estimating CoD explicitly, even roughly, turns prioritization debates from opinion contests into a shared numeric conversation the whole team can reason about.

## One Thing to Try Today

Take the top three items in your current backlog and, for each one, write a one-sentence rough estimate of its weekly Cost of Delay (lost revenue, risk exposure, or compliance penalty per week of delay). You don't need precision — even a low/medium/high tag with a one-line rationale is enough to see whether your current priority order actually matches where delay is most expensive.
