## PM Concept of the Day: Risk Appetite, Risk Tolerance, and Risk Threshold

Risk appetite is the overall amount and type of risk an organization (or stakeholder) is willing to pursue to achieve its objectives. Risk tolerance is the acceptable deviation around a specific objective, and risk threshold is the concrete measurable level at which action is triggered, such as "escalate if forecast cost overrun exceeds 10%". In PMBOK these attitudes shape how the team scores risks and which responses sponsors will fund. In fintech delivery, a payments launch might have low appetite for compliance or fraud risk but high appetite for UX experimentation, so a PM would set a threshold like "pause rollout if the chargeback rate exceeds 0.5%". For the exam, remember: appetite is the broad stance, tolerance is the allowed variance, threshold is the trigger point, and they are set by stakeholders, not invented by the PM.

## One Thing to Try Today (10 minutes)

Pick a project you know (real or imagined, such as launching a new payment method). Write three one-line risk statements, then for each define a measurable threshold and who must be told when it is crossed (for example, "fraud losses above 0.3% of volume, notify the risk officer within 24 hours"). Check whether each threshold is a number you could actually monitor.
