- **BILL agreed to be taken private by TPG, with Corpay as a minority investor, for about $6.0B (announced Nov 19).** BILL is a US company that sells accounts-payable and accounts-receivable automation software to small and mid-sized businesses. It would hold stablecoin balances, make stablecoin payments and convert them into local currencies for Payoneer's cross-border business customers. It replaces the payment institution licence Qonto has held since 2018. Qonto plans to launch invoice financing, credit lines, loans and term deposits starting in the first half of 2027. Deposits will be covered by France's €100k guarantee once the licence is in effect, but Qonto hasn't given a date for that. ## PM Concept of the Day: Rough Order of Magnitude (ROM) Estimates & the Cone of Uncertainty

A **Rough Order of Magnitude (ROM)** estimate is the wide-range estimate you give at project initiation, before scope is understood. PMBOK puts its accuracy at about **−25% to +75%**. As planning goes on it narrows to a **budget estimate** (about −10% to +25%) and then a **definitive estimate** (about −5% to +10%). The **cone of uncertainty** describes the same idea: estimates can only become accurate as decisions are made and unknowns are resolved, so committing to a precise date or cost at kickoff hides risk rather than removing it. For the PMP exam, remember those three accuracy ranges, and remember that you refine estimates through progressive elaboration instead of defending the first number. Fintech example: if you're the PM for Qonto's new lending products, a leader asking "when can we launch credit lines?" the week the licence arrives should get a ROM range such as "Q2–Q4 2027." That range should state the assumptions that will narrow it, such as when the licence takes effect, when the supervisor approves the credit-risk models, and when the core-banking vendor delivers the loan ledger. Then give a date range with stated confidence after discovery, rather than a single date that regulatory and vendor dependencies are likely to break.

## One Thing to Try Today (10 minutes)

Choose a feature you know, for example "add Apple Pay to our checkout." Write one ROM estimate for it as a range, such as "6–14 weeks," followed by exactly three assumptions that would move the estimate to either end of the range. For each assumption, write the single question or spike that would confirm or rule it out. That gives you a ready answer to "how long will it take?" that is honest about uncertainty and that people can act on.
