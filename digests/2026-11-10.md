## PM Concept of the Day: NPV and Payback Period (Project Selection Methods)

Net Present Value (NPV) discounts a project's future cash flows to today's money and subtracts the initial investment. Choose projects with a positive NPV, and when comparing options, the higher NPV wins. Payback period is simpler: it measures how long the project takes to recover its cost, and it ignores the time value of money and any cash flows after breakeven. On the PMP exam these are "benefit measurement methods" used in business-case and project-selection questions, and a typical question asks which of two projects to pick. In fintech, a team weighing a custom fraud-detection build against a vendor license can compare both over three years. The build may have a higher upfront cost and a better NPV, while the vendor option pays back sooner but costs more over time. A PM who presents both numbers lets sponsors choose between speed and long-term value.

## One Thing to Try Today (10 minutes)

Pick a feature your team shipped or is considering. Estimate its upfront cost and its yearly benefit for three years, using any rough numbers. Compute the payback period, then the NPV at a 10% discount rate in a spreadsheet. Write one sentence on which number would change your recommendation, and why.
