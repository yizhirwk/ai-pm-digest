# PM + Fintech Daily Digest — 2026-09-03

## Fintech Industry News

1. **Félix Pago raises $200M to scale WhatsApp-based remittances.** The Miami-based startup closed an $87M Series C led by Andreessen Horowitz (with QED Investors participating) plus a $113M credit facility from General Catalyst's Customer Value Fund. Félix lets Latin American immigrants in the US send money home over WhatsApp, settling transfers on USDC/blockchain rails behind the scenes so users never have to touch a crypto wallet directly.

2. **21 major financial institutions commit to a joint stablecoin venture.** The consortium announced plans to launch a new company in H2 2026 to issue a USD-denominated stablecoin, with longer-term ambitions to extend into other G7 currencies including the euro. It signals traditional banks moving to build shared stablecoin infrastructure themselves rather than cede that rail entirely to crypto-native issuers.

3. **SBI pays $270M for a 20% stake in Ajaib.** The Japanese financial giant's investment in the Indonesian multi-asset investment platform reflects continued cross-border appetite for Southeast Asia's retail investing and wealth-tech growth, even as fintech funding elsewhere stays lumpy and deal-driven.

4. **Regulators tighten scrutiny of banking-as-a-service partnerships.** Sponsor banks are increasingly being held directly accountable for the compliance failures of the fintech companies they partner with under BaaS arrangements, pushing both sides toward more rigorous, ongoing oversight rather than one-time onboarding due diligence.

## PM Concept of the Day: Third-Party / Vendor Risk Management

Third-Party (Vendor) Risk Management is the practice of continuously assessing, monitoring, and holding external partners accountable for the risks — security, compliance, delivery, financial — they introduce into your program, rather than treating vendor selection as a one-time procurement decision. It typically shows up as formal SLAs, periodic audits, defined escalation paths, and recurring risk-scoring cadences, and it sits at the intersection of the Risk Management and Procurement Management knowledge areas on the PMP exam. Item 4 above is a live example: regulators now hold sponsor banks directly accountable for their fintech partners' compliance failures under BaaS arrangements, which means the "vendor" relationship can no longer be managed with a signed contract and an annual check-in. For an IT/fintech PM, this means building ongoing monitoring — not just onboarding checklists — into any plan that depends on an external banking, KYC, or payments-rail partner.

## One Thing to Try Today

Spend 10-15 minutes drafting a simple vendor risk scorecard template with four columns — partner name, risk category (security / compliance / delivery / financial), last review date, and escalation contact — and fill in one row for a real or hypothetical partner your team depends on (a payments processor, a KYC vendor, a cloud provider). Notice whether you can actually answer all four columns from memory, or whether that gap is itself a risk worth flagging.
