## PM Concept of the Day: Acceptance Criteria & Given-When-Then (BDD)

Acceptance criteria are the specific, testable conditions a user story must meet to be accepted by the product owner, and the Given-When-Then format (from Behavior-Driven Development) writes them as scenarios: *Given* a starting context, *When* an action occurs, *Then* an observable outcome follows. They turn vague requirements into shared, verifiable examples that business, engineering, and QA all read the same way, and they feed directly into the Definition of Done and test automation. In fintech, this is especially valuable for compliance-heavy flows: for an AI-assisted onboarding project like the Customers Bank/Palantir partnership, a criterion such as "Given an applicant whose ID document fails verification, When the check completes, Then the case is routed to a human reviewer and the reason is logged" pins down escalation and audit-trail behavior before any code is written. On Agile exams, remember that acceptance criteria are written per story and agreed before the sprint, while the Definition of Done applies uniformly to all stories.

## One Thing to Try Today

Pick one story from today's news (e.g., "As a compliance analyst, I want AI-flagged onboarding cases routed to me so that I can review high-risk applicants") and write three Given-When-Then acceptance criteria for it: one happy path, one failure case, and one edge case. Takes about 10 minutes.
