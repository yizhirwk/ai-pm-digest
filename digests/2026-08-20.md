## PM Concept of the Day: Definition of Done (DoD)

Definition of Done is the agreed, explicit checklist a team uses to decide whether a piece of work — a user story, a feature, a release — is actually complete, as opposed to merely "code written" or "demo-able." It's a core Scrum/Agile concept because it removes ambiguity at the worst possible moment: sprint review, when a half-finished item can quietly get counted as delivered. A good DoD is binary (each line is checkable, not a matter of opinion) and consistent across the team, so "done" means the same thing regardless of who did the work. Today's Adyen-Toast US expansion is a useful fintech example: rolling payments processing into a new country isn't "done" when the integration code merges — a real DoD there would also require money-transmission licensing confirmed, PCI compliance validated for the new region, and support/ops readiness signed off, or the team risks calling a launch complete that customers and regulators wouldn't recognize as such.

## One Thing to Try Today

Pick one feature or workstream you're currently shipping and spend 10 minutes writing its Definition of Done as a short checklist — include at least one non-code line item (compliance sign-off, documentation, monitoring/alerting in place, support team briefed). Compare it against how the team is actually tracking "done" today and note any gap.
