## Fintech News

1. **Ramp applies for a national trust bank charter with the OCC.** The spend-management fintech said on Oct 19 that it filed to establish "Ramp National Trust Bank." A trust charter would let a fintech hold and move customer funds under federal oversight instead of relying only on partner banks. It adds to the pattern of fintechs pursuing their own charters. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-19/fintech-ramp-applies-for-trust-bank-charter-with-us-regulator))

2. **UK CMA proposes forcing Apple to open up iPhone contactless payments.** On Oct 20 the regulator proposed requiring Apple to lower contactless access fees and remove technical barriers, so rival wallets and banks can compete on the iPhone. These are proposals, not final rules. If adopted, they could change the economics of mobile-wallet payments in the UK. ([US News/Reuters](https://money.usnews.com/investing/news/articles/2026-10-20/britain-seeks-to-break-apples-grip-on-contactless-payments), [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/companies/news/apple-faces-uk-regulatory-push-to-open-iphone-contactless-payment-systems-126102000844_1.html))

Both were reported secondhand by aggregators and I did not confirm them against company announcements, so treat the details as preliminary. ## PM Concept of the Day: Quality Assurance vs. Quality Control

Quality Assurance (QA) is process-focused and preventive: it audits whether the team's methods, standards and workflows will produce a good result. Quality Control (QC) is product-focused and detective: it inspects and tests actual deliverables against requirements. PMP questions often hinge on this split, since QA belongs to the "Manage Quality" process and QC to "Control Quality". In fintech delivery, QA is the peer-review standard, automated pipeline gates and compliance checklists before a payments release. QC is the regression, reconciliation and penetration tests run on the build itself, catching a rounding bug before it moves real money.

## One Thing to Try Today

Take a recent release or feature you know (or a public app's changelog) and spend 10 minutes sorting its quality activities into two columns: QA (prevent defects in the process) and QC (detect defects in the product). Note one gap in either column, and write a one-sentence suggestion to close it.
