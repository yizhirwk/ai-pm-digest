## PM Concept of the Day: Planning Poker & Relative Estimation (Story Points)

Relative estimation sizes work by comparing items to each other (e.g., "this is about twice the effort of that one") instead of predicting hours. Planning Poker applies this: each team member privately picks a Fibonacci-style card (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13…), all reveal at once to avoid anchoring, and outliers explain their reasoning before re-voting. The value is the conversation, since a developer voting 13 against a tester's 3 often exposes a hidden dependency or unclear requirement. In fintech delivery, a story like "add a new payout rail via a stablecoin provider" might split the room because of compliance screening or reconciliation work nobody had mentioned, which is far cheaper to find in refinement than mid-sprint. For the exam, remember that story points are team-specific and shouldn't be compared across teams or used as a performance metric.

## One Thing to Try Today

Take one item from today's news (say, the Mastercard–BVNK integration) and write three user stories for a bank adopting it. Then estimate each with Planning Poker–style relative sizing against a story you know well, and note which one you're least sure about and why (about 10 minutes).
