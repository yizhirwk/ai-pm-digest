# PM + Fintech Daily Digest — 2026-09-01

## Fintech Industry News

1. **Treasury/FinCEN propose the detailed rulebook for who counts as a stablecoin "issuer."** Published in the Federal Register on August 18 to implement Section 3 of the GENIUS Act, the rule defines "issuance" as the first transfer of a stablecoin that gives someone else the right to use, transfer, or redeem it (capturing indirect transfers too), and defines "issuer" as whoever is on the hook for redemption at a fixed value. It's the mechanical detail — especially relevant for white-label stablecoin arrangements — that turns last month's headline law into something compliance teams can actually build controls against.

2. **FASB proposes letting compliant stablecoins count as cash equivalents on a balance sheet.** The Financial Accounting Standards Board's draft Accounting Standards Update adds illustrative examples clarifying that a stablecoin with a direct, on-demand redemption right and segregated 1:1 reserves in short-term liquid assets can qualify under the existing cash-equivalents definition — a signal that stablecoins are being folded into standard corporate treasury accounting rather than treated as a separate, exotic asset class.

3. **U.S. Bank goes live with Fenergo's Fen-X platform for alternative-investment onboarding.** U.S. Bank Investment Services is now using the regtech platform to digitize investor onboarding for alternative-investment clients, part of a multi-year client-lifecycle-management overhaul that started with U.S. Bank Global Fund Services — a concrete example of a large incumbent bank replacing manual KYC/onboarding workflows with vendor regtech rather than building in-house.

4. **Sachin Bansal's Navi raises its first-ever institutional capital: $100M from Prosus.** The India-based digital financial services platform (UPI payments, NBFC lending, insurance, mutual funds) accepted outside investment for the first time since its 2018 founding, valuing the company at roughly $1.3 billion. The raise, still subject to Indian antitrust clearance, is explicitly framed as balance-sheet preparation ahead of a planned FY27 IPO.

5. **Weekly fintech funding trackers show a cooling, concentrated summer.** Global fintech deal activity slowed to roughly $389M across just 12 deals in the most recent tracked week — well below the $2B+ weeks seen earlier in August — with what capital is flowing still concentrated in payments infrastructure and regtech/compliance tooling rather than broad-based consumer fintech.

## PM Concept of the Day: Definition of Ready (DoR)

Definition of Ready is the companion concept to Definition of Done: a checklist a backlog item must satisfy *before* a team pulls it into a sprint — clear acceptance criteria, dependencies identified, sized/estimated, and no open questions blocking a developer from starting. Where DoD protects the exit of work, DoR protects the entry, preventing a team from committing to vague or under-specified stories that stall mid-sprint once someone actually starts building. It's a common Scrum-exam distinction because teams often only define "done" and then wonder why half their sprint items get stuck waiting on a decision. In a fintech context, a story like "add stablecoin issuer classification to onboarding" isn't ready until the team knows which Treasury/FinCEN definition of "issuer" it needs to satisfy (see item #1 above) — pulling it into a sprint before that's nailed down is how engineers end up blocked mid-sprint waiting on compliance to answer a question that should have been resolved at refinement.

## One Thing to Try Today

Pick one item currently sitting in your team's backlog (or a hypothetical one, like "support a new stablecoin type in KYC checks") and write out a real Definition of Ready checklist for it — acceptance criteria, known dependencies, open questions — before it would be allowed into a sprint. Spend 10 minutes seeing how many boxes it actually fails today; that gap is exactly what causes mid-sprint stalls.
