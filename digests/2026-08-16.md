## PM Concept of the Day: Fast-Tracking vs. Crashing (Schedule Compression)

When a project is behind schedule, PMP methodology gives you two distinct compression techniques, and knowing which to reach for matters. **Fast-tracking** means running normally sequential activities in parallel — it's free (no extra budget) but adds rework risk if the reordered tasks turn out to depend on each other after all. **Crashing** means adding resources (more staff, overtime, expedited vendor fees) to shorten specific tasks on the critical path — it costs money but doesn't inherently increase risk. RedotPay's IPO delay above is really a case where fast-tracking wasn't an option: legal and regulatory sign-off has to happen *before* a listing, not alongside it, so the company's only lever to recover time would be crashing the legal-review process (more counsel, faster regulator engagement) — at real cost. A fintech PM facing a compliance-driven slip should always ask "is this a true dependency I have to crash through, or a false one I can fast-track around?" before assuming the schedule can only be recovered by spending more.

## One Thing to Try Today

Spend 10 minutes listing the last project or task you were involved in (work or personal) and mark each step as either genuinely sequential (a true dependency) or something you *assumed* had to happen in order. For anything in the second bucket, note how you'd have fast-tracked it — this builds the instinct for spotting false dependencies before a real deadline crunch forces the question.
