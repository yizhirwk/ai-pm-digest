# Daily PM + Fintech Digest — 2026-09-24

## Fintech News Roundup

- **Visa survey: stablecoin adoption intent nearly doubles with bank-level protections.** Visa reported on September 23 that consumer interest in using stablecoins jumps from 36% to 56% when paired with hypothetical fraud protection and deposit insurance — a signal that trust infrastructure, not just rails, is the real adoption bottleneck for consumer stablecoin products.
- **India puts a price on large UPI transactions.** India's Unified Payments Interface will impose a 0.4% merchant fee on payments above ₹2,000 (about $21) starting October 15, ending years of fee-free UPI transfers for larger purchases and reshaping merchant economics on the world's largest real-time payments network.
- **Mastercard and Google Pay add biometric authentication for card payments in India.** The companies plan to let users authenticate eligible card payments (utility bills, mobile recharges, online checkout) with biometrics by the end of 2026, part of a broader push to reduce OTP friction and fraud in embedded payment flows.
- **AI is raising the security stakes for tax and compliance workflows.** A September 23 report highlighted how AI-powered compliance tooling is expanding auditors' and regtechs' attack surface even as it speeds up reviews, reinforcing why frameworks like SEC's FY2026 exam priorities and FINRA's 2026 oversight report are elevating GenAI and cyber-fraud risk this year.
- **Citi builds an advertising business on top of transaction data.** Citi is turning consumer spending data into a new ad-targeting product, joining a wider trend of banks and card networks monetizing payment data directly — a move that will draw fresh scrutiny on data-privacy and consent practices in banking-as-a-service arrangements.

## PM Concept of the Day: Definition of Done (DoD)

Definition of Done is a shared, team-agreed checklist that specifies exactly what "complete" means for any piece of work — not just "code written," but code reviewed, tested, documented, and deployable. It's one of the most heavily tested Agile/Scrum concepts on PMP and CSM-style exams because it directly prevents the classic failure mode of a team calling a story "done" when it's actually 80% done and quietly accumulating hidden work. In fintech delivery, a weak Definition of Done is dangerous in a specific way: a payments feature or KYC change can look "done" from an engineering standpoint while missing a compliance sign-off, an audit-logging requirement, or a security review — exactly the kind of gap that surfaces during an external audit rather than a sprint review. A strong fintech DoD makes those regulatory and security checks explicit line items, not assumed steps, so "done" means the same thing to engineering, compliance, and the auditor.

## One Thing to Try Today

Pull up your team's current Definition of Done (or write one from scratch if none exists) and check whether it explicitly lists compliance, security, or audit-logging steps as separate checklist items — not folded into a vague "meets acceptance criteria" line. If they're missing, draft the one or two lines that would make them explicit and share them with your team lead for the next sprint planning session.
