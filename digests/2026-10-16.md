## PM Concept of the Day: Control Charts (Quality Management)

A control chart plots a process metric over time against a centerline (the mean) and upper and lower control limits, typically set at three standard deviations. Points inside the limits reflect normal "common cause" variation, while points outside them, or a run of seven or more on one side of the mean (the "rule of seven"), signal a "special cause" that deserves investigation. PMP exams test the difference between control limits (what the process actually does) and specification limits (what the customer requires). In fintech delivery, plot payment-API p95 latency or daily failed-transaction rates: a lone spike after a release is a special cause to trace, while ordinary day-to-day wobble should not trigger a rollback.

## One Thing to Try Today

Take any metric you can see (build times, ticket cycle time, or even your daily step count) and pull the last 15-20 data points into a spreadsheet. Spend 10 minutes computing the mean and plotting lines at mean ± 3 standard deviations. Note which points, if any, count as special causes.
