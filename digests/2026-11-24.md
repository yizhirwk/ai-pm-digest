## Fintech News

Today's one confirmed story is South Korea's draft crypto law, which is due for release this afternoon (Seoul time). The other two items were announced Nov 19 but haven't appeared in earlier digests. Larger stories from the past week (BILL's ~$6B take-private, Payhawk's $280M Series C, the World Bank stablecoin pilot, Payoneer's OCC trust-bank approval) were already covered on Nov 18–23.

- **South Korea's Financial Services Commission is presenting its draft Digital Asset Basic Act today (Nov 24).** The bill is roughly 135 articles. It would move crypto businesses from simple registration to full licensing, create a separate custodian license, and set rules for won-denominated stablecoins. Most reports say issuers would have to be bank-led consortia, with banks holding more than 50%, which is what the Bank of Korea wants. One report (Seoul Shinmun, via Bloomingbit) says fintech firms could be the largest shareholders instead, so treat the ownership rules as unconfirmed until the official text is out. After the draft is published it will be merged with lawmakers' bills, and the ruling party wants it passed by January. ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-11-23-south-korea-to-publish-draft-digital-asset-basic-act-444881), [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/south-korea-crypto-law-draft-november-24-stablecoins/), [Bloomingbit](https://en.bloomingbit.io/feed/news/124998), [Blockhead](https://www.blockhead.co/2026/11/12/koreas-digital-asset-basic-act-lands-this-month/))
- **Tether and Abu Dhabi's Further Asset Management put about $10M into MetaComp, a Singapore stablecoin-payments firm (announced Nov 19).** The money extends MetaComp's Pre-A+ round to about $45M in total. It will go toward compliance infrastructure and new payment corridors connecting Asia, the Middle East and Africa. The deal shows stablecoin issuers buying stakes in licensed payment companies that move their tokens across borders. ([Chainwire release](https://chainwire.org/2026/11/19/metacomp-extends-pre-a-with-approximately-us10m-from-tether-and-further/), [Macau Business](https://macaubusiness.com/metacomp-extends-pre-a-with-approximately-us10m-from-tether-and-further/), [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/metacomp-adds-about-10-million-from-tether-and-further/))

Finexio pays suppliers on companies' behalf by virtual card, ACH or check. Together they cover both incoming and outgoing B2B payments. Finexio will keep its brand, and the deal still has to meet customary closing conditions. It's the same idea behind BILL's sale last week: B2B payments firms are combining so one platform can handle both money in and money out. ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/174456-checkalt-to-acquire-payables-firm-finexio-adding-supplier-payments/), [Startup Researcher](https://www.startupresearcher.com/news/checkalt-to-acquire-finexio))

## PM Concept of the Day: Agile Release Planning

**Release planning** turns a product backlog into a forecast of what will ship across several sprints, usually 3–6 months out. The inputs are the prioritized backlog, the team's measured velocity (or throughput), the release goal, and any fixed dates. The output is a release plan: a target date, a range of scope you expect to deliver by then (often split into "will," "likely," and "stretch" bands), and the main dependencies and risks. The key choice is what you hold fixed. When the date can't move, you fix the date and let scope flex, and you protect the must-have scope with a buffer. You re-forecast at the end of every sprint and don't treat the plan as a promise. Fintech tie-in: a regulatory date like the GENIUS Act's Jan 18, 2027 effective date, or Korea's licensing regime once it passes, can't slip. A good release plan puts the compliance must-haves (licensing evidence, reserve reporting, redemption flows) inside what the team can reliably deliver by that date. Nice-to-haves such as new wallet UX sit in the stretch band, so when velocity drops, they get cut and the compliance work still ships.

## One Thing to Try Today (10–15 minutes)

Make a **fixed-date release plan** for an imaginary "Korea won-stablecoin readiness" project. In a spreadsheet, list 10 backlog items, each with a story-point estimate (e.g., custodian segregation, licensing application pack, KYC for issuers, reserve attestation report, redemption API, merchant dashboard). Assume a velocity of 20 points per two-week sprint and a go-live 4 sprints away (80 points of capacity). Draw a line at about 70% of capacity (56 points), at 100% (80 points), and above 100%. Put compliance-critical items in the first band, "likely" items in the second, and stretch items above the line. Then write one sentence on what you'd cut first if velocity fell to 15. Save it as an interview example of planning around a regulatory deadline.
