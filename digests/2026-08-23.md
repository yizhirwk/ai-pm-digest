## PM Concept of the Day: Cost of Delay & WSJF (Weighted Shortest Job First)

Cost of Delay quantifies what it costs an organization, per unit of time, to *not* ship a given piece of work — turning "this feels urgent" into a comparable number. WSJF, a prioritization technique from Scaled Agile (SAFe), divides Cost of Delay (business value + time criticality + risk reduction/opportunity enablement) by job size, so smaller items that unlock large value or reduce large risk quickly bubble to the top of the backlog. This is exactly the calculus fintech PMs face right now with the OCC's GENIUS Act timeline: work that gets a bank or payment company ready to apply for a stablecoin issuer license the moment applications open in 2027 has a high time-criticality score (miss the window and a competitor captures the market first) and arguably belongs above feature work with comparable size but no hard external deadline. Unlike MoSCoW or a simple risk register, WSJF forces an explicit trade-off between competing "must-do" items when everything can't be done at once — useful when a compliance deadline, a customer commitment, and a technical-debt fix are all fighting for the same sprint.

## One Thing to Try Today

Take the top 3-5 items in a backlog you're responsible for (or a personal project list) and score each 1-10 on business value, time criticality, and risk reduction, then divide the sum by a rough size/effort estimate (1-10). Rank them by that score — spend 10 minutes comparing the resulting order to your gut-feel priority list and note where a hard external deadline (like a regulatory cutoff) should be pulling an item higher than its "size" alone would suggest.
