## Fintech News

- **TabaPay raises $155M and plans to acquire Transact Bank, aiming to become "TabaBank."** The strategic growth round, led by FTV Capital, backs TabaPay's plan to acquire the OCC-chartered, FDIC-insured Transact Bank N.A. and fold it into a new bank holding company — a vertical-integration move that would let the payments processor pair its RTP/FedNow/ACH money-movement rails with direct bank sponsorship instead of relying on partner banks.

- **Singapore's MAS opens consultation on stablecoin legislative changes.** The proposed amendments to the Payment Services Act 2019 would formalize how stablecoin issuers qualify for MAS regulation, adding Singapore to the growing list of jurisdictions moving stablecoins from crypto-adjacent grey area into licensed, reserve-backed regimes.

## PM Concept of the Day: RAID Log (Risks, Assumptions, Issues, Dependencies)

A RAID log is a single, living document that tracks a project's Risks (things that might happen and hurt the project), Assumptions (things the team is treating as true without proof), Issues (things that have already happened and need resolution), and Dependencies (external factors or teams the project's timeline relies on). It's a lightweight complement to a formal risk register — easier to maintain in a fast-moving Agile environment, but still exam-relevant for PMP as a core monitoring-and-controlling artifact. In fintech delivery, a RAID log earns its keep fast: an "assumption" that a partner bank's sandbox environment behaves like production, or a "dependency" on a regulator's approval timeline (like the FCA's stablecoin authorization window or MAS's PSA consultation), can quietly become the item that blocks a launch if nobody wrote it down and revisited it. The discipline isn't the template — it's the weekly habit of re-reading each entry and asking whether an assumption has since been validated, a dependency has slipped, or a risk has crossed over into becoming an issue.

## One Thing to Try Today

Open a blank note and write one RAID entry for a real project you're involved in (or observing) — pick just one item you're currently *assuming* is true (e.g., "the vendor's API will be ready by go-live," "compliance sign-off takes one week"). Write down what would have to happen for that assumption to be wrong, and who you'd need to ask to confirm it. This is the core reflex a RAID log is meant to build: turning an unspoken belief into a tracked, checkable item.
