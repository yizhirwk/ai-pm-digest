## PM Concept of the Day: Organizational Structures (Functional, Matrix, Projectized)

PMI ties a project manager's authority to the organization's structure. In a **functional** organization, department heads control people and budget, and the PM acts as a part-time coordinator with little authority. In a **projectized** organization, the PM has full authority and the team reports to them. **Matrix** organizations sit between the two: in a *weak* matrix the functional manager has more power, in a *balanced* matrix the PM and functional manager share it, and in a *strong* matrix the PM has more. A common exam trap: if a question says resources are pulled away by their line managers and the PM has to negotiate for people, the setting is functional or weak matrix, and the right answer is to negotiate and escalate, not to give orders. Fintech tie-in: the UniCredit–Commerzbank integration will almost certainly run as a strong matrix. Core-banking, payments, and compliance specialists still report to their own bank's line managers, but integration PMs have to secure their time against fixed cutover dates. Working out who controls each person's priorities early is what keeps the migration on schedule.

## One Thing to Try Today (10 minutes)

Think of your current job, or a company you'd like to join, and name its structure: functional, weak/balanced/strong matrix, or projectized. Then write three short lines: (1) who sets a project team member's priorities day to day, (2) who approves the project budget, and (3) one thing a PM there would have to *negotiate* rather than decide. Keep the note. It answers the interview question "How do you get things done without direct authority?" with a concrete example. It also helps with the PMP questions that turn on organizational structure.
