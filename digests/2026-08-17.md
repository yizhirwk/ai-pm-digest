## PM Concept of the Day: MoSCoW Prioritization

MoSCoW is a requirements-prioritization technique that sorts backlog items into **Must have, Should have, Could have,** and **Won't have (this time)** — forcing explicit trade-off conversations instead of treating every request as equally urgent. It's especially useful in fintech delivery, where a "Must have" is often dictated by a regulatory deadline (e.g., a mandatory AML control) while a "Could have" might be a UX polish item that can slip a release without real consequence. On the PMP exam, MoSCoW shows up under scope and requirements management as a lightweight alternative to more numeric prioritization schemes like weighted scoring. The discipline it enforces — naming what you will *not* build this cycle — is often more valuable to stakeholders than the "Must have" list itself, since it surfaces disagreements early.

## One Thing to Try Today

Pick a real or hypothetical fintech feature (e.g., "add instant payment support" or "add a fraud-alert dashboard") and spend 10 minutes sorting a rough list of 6-8 requirements into Must/Should/Could/Won't using MoSCoW — then write one sentence justifying your single most controversial "Won't have" call.
