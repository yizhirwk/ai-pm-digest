# PM + Fintech Daily Digest — 2026-08-19

## Fintech News

- **US Treasury opens comment period on GENIUS Act stablecoin rules.** On August 17, 2026, Treasury issued a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking on Section 3 of the GENIUS Act, seeking public input on exactly when a person "issues" or "offers/sells" a payment stablecoin to a US person — the threshold question for who needs a license. The 60-day comment window pushes final clarity closer to the Act's January 18, 2027 effective date, keeping issuers and their compliance teams in a holding pattern a bit longer.
- **EU AI Act's high-risk rules hit full enforcement for fintech credit models.** As of August 2, 2026, AI-driven credit scoring, insurance risk pricing, and AML risk profiling are formally classified as "high-risk" under Annex III, triggering mandatory conformity assessments, technical documentation, and human-oversight obligations — with penalties up to €35m or 7% of global turnover for the strictest violations. Banks and fintechs training or materially modifying their own scoring models carry the heaviest documentation burden; those merely deploying an unmodified third-party model face lighter but still real oversight duties.
- **Airwallex and Affirm embed pay-over-time financing at checkout.** Announced August 13, 2026, the partnership lets merchants already on Airwallex's platform offer Affirm's installment options — including interest-free 4-payment plans and terms up to 24 months — to eligible US shoppers without a separate BNPL integration. It's another sign payments infrastructure players are racing to bundle credit products directly into checkout rather than leaving merchants to bolt them on.
- **Weekly fintech funding tops $800m, led by Team8's new fund.** FinTech Global's roundup for the week of August 14 recorded roughly $808m raised across a dozen deals, anchored by cybersecurity-and-fintech venture builder Team8's $365m raise (a $265m third fund plus $100m+ for follow-on investment in existing portfolio companies) — with US-based deals dominating at eight of the twelve.

## PM Concept of the Day: Risk Register & Probability-Impact Matrix

A risk register is the running log every PM keeps of identified project risks, each rated on a probability-impact matrix — likelihood of occurring crossed with severity if it does — to produce a priority score that decides which risks get active mitigation plans versus passive monitoring. It's a foundational PMP risk-management artifact precisely because it forces a team to name uncertainty explicitly rather than discover it as a crisis. Today's Treasury GENIUS Act comment period is a textbook example of a risk that should already be sitting in a fintech compliance PM's register: "final Section 3 licensing rules slip past our target ship date" is a known-unknown with real probability (rulemaking has already missed one statutory deadline this year) and real impact (a stablecoin product launch gated on licensing clarity). A PM running a stablecoin or AI-credit-scoring workstream should have logged that risk months ago, assigned it an owner, and built a response plan — accept the delay, escalate for a contingency timeline, or de-scope the launch — rather than treating today's NPRM as a surprise.

## One Thing to Try Today

Spend 10 minutes building a bare-bones risk register for a project you're on: list 3-5 risks, score each 1-5 on probability and 1-5 on impact, multiply for a priority score, and sort. Notice which risk lands at the top — if you haven't already got a mitigation plan for it, that's your next action item.
