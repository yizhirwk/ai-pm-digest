## PM Concept of the Day: Operational Readiness Review & the Go/No-Go Decision

An **operational readiness review (ORR)** is a structured checkpoint just before go-live. It asks whether the organization is ready to *run* the new system, which is a separate question from whether the software is finished. The checklist usually covers trained support staff, runbooks and on-call rotas, monitoring and alerting, a tested rollback plan, sign-off from compliance and risk, and clear communications to customers and partners. The review ends in a **go/no-go decision** made by named accountable owners against criteria agreed *in advance*, so that schedule pressure on launch day can't quietly lower the bar. Fintech tie-in: OKXICE's launch shows why an ORR looks beyond the code. A venue can be technically ready while key external conditions are missing, such as issuer cooperation and liquidity providers. A good go/no-go checklist names these explicitly, decides beforehand whether each one is a "must-have" or an "accepted risk," and sets the post-launch metrics (e.g., daily volume and spreads) that would trigger a rethink.

## One Thing to Try Today (10–15 minutes)

Write a **one-page go/no-go checklist** for an imaginary "24/7 tokenized-stock trading launch." Make three columns: *Criterion*, *Owner*, *Must-have or accepted risk*. Fill in 8–10 rows that span technology (load test passed, rollback rehearsed), operations (24/7 support rota staffed), compliance (exemption conditions met, disclosures published), and the market (at least one liquidity provider signed, issuer objections reviewed by legal). Then mark the two rows you'd be willing to launch without and write one sentence explaining why. Keep it as an interview example that shows you can separate "code complete" from "ready to operate."
