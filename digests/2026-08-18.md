## PM Concept of the Day: Stakeholder Power/Interest Grid

A power/interest grid is a stakeholder-analysis tool that plots every stakeholder on two axes — how much power they hold over the project's outcome, and how much interest they have in it — sorting them into four quadrants that dictate engagement strategy: manage closely (high power, high interest), keep satisfied (high power, low interest), keep informed (low power, high interest), and monitor with minimal effort (low power, low interest). It's a core PMP stakeholder-management tool because treating every stakeholder identically wastes effort on people who don't need it and under-invests in the ones who can kill a project. The Bunq charter denial above is a clean illustration: the OCC is a textbook "manage closely" stakeholder — maximum power (it can simply refuse the license) and maximum interest (safety-and-soundness is its mandate) — yet Bunq's own account of the process suggests its US capital and staffing plans weren't built with that regulator's specific concerns front and center. A fintech PM running a charter or major regulatory-filing program should map the reviewing regulator into this grid on day one and tailor the entire submission and communication plan around what that specific "manage closely" stakeholder is known to scrutinize, rather than treating the filing as a generic paperwork exercise.

## One Thing to Try Today

Spend 10 minutes drafting a power/interest grid for a project you're currently involved in (work, school, or a personal initiative) — list every stakeholder, place each in one of the four quadrants, and write one sentence per "manage closely" stakeholder on what specifically they care about. This is a fast way to catch a high-power stakeholder you've been under-communicating with before it becomes a problem.
