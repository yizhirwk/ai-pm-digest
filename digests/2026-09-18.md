# Daily PM + Fintech Digest — 2026-09-18

## Fintech News

- **FDIC proposes a fast-track overhaul of bank merger reviews.** On September 17 the FDIC board approved a notice of proposed rulemaking that would sort merger applications into processing tiers — including a de minimis pipeline resolved in as little as five business days — with 90- or 180-day timelines for standard deals and a 270-day maximum, replacing a slower, less predictable framework built for an older banking landscape.
- **The House passes the FUTURES Act 417–7 to modernize financial regulators' technology.** The bipartisan bill (Stutzman/Foster) requires agencies like the Fed, CFPB, and FDIC to report their technical capabilities to Congress, identify technology gaps versus the industry they supervise, and reassess every five years — a direct response to concerns that supervisory tooling has fallen behind the fintechs and banks it's meant to oversee.
- **Stablecoin-infrastructure startup Velocity raises $10M with Circle Ventures, Ripple, and a new Visa Ventures backer.** The round funds a stablecoin payments and treasury platform serving issuers, acquirers, and financial institutions, underscoring how "boring" plumbing — custody, settlement, treasury — rather than the tokens themselves is becoming the real competitive layer in stablecoins.
- **Stablecoin supply hit $303B in August with $401–527B in identified payments volume year-to-date (Allium).** The data point reinforces a 2026 theme: stablecoins are shifting from speculative crypto assets into genuine payment rails at meaningful transaction scale.
- **EBA opens consultation on the €30bn threshold that forces investment firms to become full banks, as ASIC cuts licensing turnaround to 120 days.** Two regulators moving in the same direction — Europe reconsidering where the "become a bank" line sits, Australia speeding up approvals ahead of a digital-asset platform deadline — both signal continued global pressure to make fintech-to-bank pathways faster and clearer.

## PM Concept of the Day: PI Planning (Program Increment Planning)

PI Planning is the cadence-based, big-room planning event at the heart of the Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe): every 8-12 weeks, all the teams on an Agile Release Train come together (in person or virtually) to align on a shared vision, surface cross-team dependencies, and commit to a set of objectives for the next Program Increment. It's a SAFe/scaled-Agile exam staple because it solves a problem single-team Scrum doesn't address — coordinating delivery across many teams working on one product or platform. In fintech, this is exactly the situation when a core banking migration, a new payments rail, or a regulatory deadline (like a BaaS sponsor bank's compliance overhaul) requires the mobile team, the compliance/regtech team, and the payments-infrastructure team to ship in lockstep. The output isn't just a schedule — it's a visible program board of cross-team dependencies and a shared confidence vote, so a blocked dependency surfaces in the planning room instead of three sprints later during an audit.

## One Thing to Try Today

Pick one initiative you're aware of (at work or from today's news, like a bank merger or a BaaS compliance overhaul) that clearly touches more than one team. Spend 10 minutes sketching just the cross-team dependencies on paper — which team is waiting on which other team, and for what — the same exercise a PI Planning "program board" is built from. Notice how much of that dependency map already exists only in people's heads.
