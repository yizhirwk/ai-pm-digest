# Daily PM + Fintech Digest — 2026-09-25

## Fintech News Roundup

- **The Fed proposes formal stablecoin rules under the GENIUS Act.** On September 24, the Federal Reserve opened public comment on two proposals for Board-supervised payment stablecoin issuers: full 1:1 backing with reserves like short-term Treasuries, plus standardized capital charges (roughly 1-2%) for credit and operational risk — the first concrete prudential framework since GENIUS became law.
- **Avant files for a national bank charter.** The consumer lending/credit platform filed with the OCC and FDIC on September 18 to establish Avant Bank, N.A., joining a growing line of fintechs (including Cash App's parent) seeking bank charters so they can control the full customer deposit and credit experience directly instead of relying on a partner bank.
- **Six major banks publish joint principles for "agentic commerce."** Bank of America, Capital One, NatWest, ING, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, and ASB Bank released a five-principle framework (transparency, safety, privacy, choice, interoperability) on September 23 for how AI shopping agents should identify themselves and transact on a customer's behalf — a voluntary first move ahead of expected regulation as agentic payments scale.
- **Fintech funding stayed strong going into Q4.** Fintech.global tracked roughly $858m raised across deals in the past week, led by UAE buy-now-pay-later player Tabby ($233m) and on-chain data firm Kaiko ($110m), with US and UK deal counts leading geographically — a sign investor appetite hasn't cooled even as later-stage rounds get more selective.

## PM Concept of the Day: Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) Framework

Jobs-to-be-Done reframes product discovery around the underlying "job" a customer is trying to get done, rather than their stated feature requests — the classic formulation is that people don't want a drill, they want a hole in the wall. It shows up on product-management interviews and Agile discovery training because it forces a PM to separate the functional job (what task is being accomplished) from the emotional and social jobs (how the person wants to feel, and how they want to be seen) that shape which solution actually gets adopted. In fintech, JTBD is especially useful for products like real-time payments or fraud alerts: the functional job might be "move money instantly," but the real job is often "know with certainty that my money arrived and I won't get blindsided by a hidden fee or a declined transaction." Framing backlog items around the job instead of a feature request keeps a team from building a technically correct feature that customers still don't use, because it solved the wrong job.

## One Thing to Try Today

Pick one feature currently in your backlog or roadmap and write a single "job story" for it in the format: "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]." Compare it against the feature's existing ticket description — if the job story reveals a different underlying need than what's written, flag it for discussion at your next backlog refinement.
