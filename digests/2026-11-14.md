## PM Concept of the Day: Residual Risks vs. Secondary Risks

PMBOK separates two kinds of risk that remain after you respond to a risk:

- **Residual risk** is the exposure left over after a planned response is in place. Because you expect it, you usually cover it with contingency reserve or simply accept it.
- **Secondary risk** is a *new* risk created by the response itself. It needs its own entry in the risk register, with an owner and a response.

Exam questions often test whether you can tell them apart. For example: "you outsourced a component to transfer risk, and now the vendor might go bankrupt." That vendor risk is a secondary risk.

The Intermex deal is a real-world case. Western Union's response to regulatory risk was to file for state-by-state approvals and keep extending them. The residual risk was one holdout regulator. The secondary risk was that approval would come with conditions forcing material changes to the business. On a fintech delivery project the pattern is the same. If you add a second payment processor as a failover to mitigate an outage, you also create secondary risks: reconciliation mismatches across two settlement files, and a doubled PCI scope. Those need to be logged and owned, not just celebrated as "risk mitigated."

## One Thing to Try Today (10 minutes)

Take one risk response you've seen or can imagine on a payments or banking project, such as "add a backup KYC vendor," "use feature flags for the rollout" or "outsource card issuing to a BaaS partner." Draw a three-column table:

1. **Original risk**
2. **Residual risk:** what exposure remains after the response?
3. **Secondary risk:** what new risk does the response itself create?

Fill in at least two secondary risks. Then write one sentence on how you would respond to the most serious one. Being able to say "this mitigation introduces X, and here's how we'll handle it" in a steering meeting is a strong signal of PM maturity.
