## PM Concept of the Day: Burndown vs. Burnup Charts

A burndown chart plots the work remaining in a sprint or release against time, falling toward zero, while a burnup chart plots work completed rising toward a separate line showing total scope. Both are standard Agile and PMP exam visuals. The difference matters when scope changes: on a burndown, added scope looks like slow progress, while on a burnup the scope line visibly steps up and the cause is clear. In fintech delivery, a payments-launch team facing late regulator-driven requirements (say, new AML checks after a consent order like SEB's) would use a burnup chart to show sponsors that the team kept its pace but the target moved. Burndown suits a stable, short sprint, and burnup suits a long release with a shifting backlog.

## One Thing to Try Today

Open a backlog or task list you have access to (or a personal project) and spend 10 minutes sketching a burnup chart on paper: draw a total-scope line and a completed-work line for the last four weeks, marking any week where scope was added. Note what story it tells that a simple "% done" would hide.
